## ⚠️ Read this first — one planning constraint that changes Session 3

**Gemini's side panel inside Google Docs, Sheets, Slides and Gmail is *not* available on free personal Gmail accounts.** It requires a paid Google Workspace or Google AI plan. If you plan to teach "Ask Gemini" buttons inside Docs and Sheets, most of the room will not see that button and the session will stall.

**What to do instead — everything below is free on a personal Gmail account:**

| Image generation, audio overviews, quizzes/flashcards, slide generation | |

The bridge that makes the whole day work is **copy-out / paste-back**: work in Gemini, then export to Docs or paste back into Sheets. Teach that bridge explicitly and the free-account limitation stops being a problem — it becomes the workflow.

## 1. At a glance

### Learning outcomes
By 16:00, every participant can independently:

1. Organise work in Google Drive with sensible folders, naming and sharing permissions.
2. Create and co-edit a document in Docs, using comments, suggestions and version history.
3. Build a data set in Sheets and answer a question with sort, filter, four formulas, a pivot table and a chart.
4. Design a Google Form, collect live responses, and connect them to Sheets automatically.
5. Chain Form → Sheets → Docs → Slides → Gmail/Calendar into one repeatable workflow.
6. Write an effective prompt using a 4-part structure and use Gemini Canvas and Gems.
7. Build a NotebookLM notebook grounded in their own sources and get cited answers from it.
8. State what workplace information must **never** be pasted into a free consumer AI tool.
9. Re-run their Session 2 workflow with AI assistance and judge where AI helped and where it did not.

### Design principles used
- **One scenario runs through the whole day** (see §3). Beginners retain workflows, not feature tours.
- **AI appears in the first 30 minutes**, not only after lunch. The poster sold AI; don't make people wait five hours for it.
- **Nothing is demonstrated that participants cannot do on their own account.**
- **Every block ends with a visible artefact** the participant has produced.
- **Maximum 20-minute teaching blocks**, each followed by hands-on time.

## 3. The through-line scenario

Everything the room builds today belongs to one story. Announce it in the first five minutes and never leave it.

> **"You have been asked to find out what training your 60 staff actually need, and to give the management a recommendation by Friday."**

| Session | What the scenario produces |
|---|---|
| **1** | A shared Drive folder, a one-page project brief in Docs, and the raw staff list in Sheets |

## 5. SESSION 1 — Your Google workspace, properly set up
**09:00–10:45 · 105 minutes**

**Session goal:** stop treating Google apps as "free Microsoft Office in a browser" and start treating Drive as one connected system.

### 5.1 · 09:00–09:10 · Welcome and framing (10 min)
- Introductions kept to: *name, organisation, one task you repeat every week that you wish took less time.* Capture these on a flipchart — you will call back to them all day.
- State the three outcomes in plain language: **organise, connect, accelerate.**
- Ground rules: hands off keyboards when you are demonstrating; ask immediately, not at the end; nobody leaves a block without a working file.
- Introduce the scenario (§3).

**Facilitator note:** ask for a show of hands — "who has used Google Docs before?" and "who has used any AI chat tool before?" Adjust your pacing to the *second-lowest* hand in the room, not the average.

### 5.2 · 09:10–09:25 · Gate check completion and pairing (15 min)
- Finish any unresolved red stickies from registration.
- Formally pair every participant into a **buddy pair** for the whole day. Rule: *before you raise your hand, ask your buddy.* This roughly halves the support load on you.
- Everyone opens drive.google.com and creates one folder: `Training Needs 2026`.

### 5.3 · 09:25–09:35 · The AI hook — 10 minutes, no hands-on (10 min)
Pure demonstration. Do not let them follow along yet — this is a promise, not a lesson.

1. Open NotebookLM, drop in your fictional Annual Report PDF, and generate an **Audio Overview**. Start it playing.
2. While it generates, switch to Gemini and paste a messy paragraph of staff feedback. Ask it to turn it into a table of themes.
3. Play 30 seconds of the audio discussion of a document nobody in the room wrote.

Then say: *"That is where we finish at 15:30. To get there, we first have to get our files in order — because AI is only as good as what you feed it."*

**Why this is here:** delaying all AI to Session 3 wastes the room's peak morning attention on menu tours, and risks people leaving at lunch having seen none of what they paid for.

### 5.4 · 09:35–09:55 · Google Drive — the foundation (20 min)
**Teach (8 min) · Practise (12 min)**

Teach:
- Drive is storage **and** the home of every app. Files are not "in Docs", they are in Drive.
- 15 GB free, shared across Drive + Gmail + Photos.
- Upload vs. **convert on upload** — dragging in a `.docx` keeps it as Word unless you open it with Docs. Show *Settings → Convert uploads*.
- Search is the real navigation: search by type, owner and date, not by remembering folders.
- **Sharing and permissions** — the most consequential 5 minutes of the morning:
  - Viewer / Commenter / Editor — what each can actually do
  - "Restricted" vs "Anyone with the link"
  - **Ownership**: if you leave the organisation, files you created leave with you
  - Share a *folder*, not fifteen individual files

Practise:
- Create `Training Needs 2026` with sub-folders `01 Raw Data`, `02 Reports`, `03 Final`.
- Upload the provided Word and PDF files; convert the Word file to Docs; note that the PDF stays a PDF.
- Share `Training Needs 2026` with your buddy as **Commenter**. Confirm with your buddy that they can see it but cannot edit.
- Find a file using search-by-type instead of clicking through folders.

**Common beginner errors to pre-empt out loud:**
- Emailing attachments back and forth instead of sharing a link → version chaos.
- Setting "Anyone with the link — Editor" on everything.
- Assuming Drive files are backed up on the laptop. They are not, unless Drive for Desktop is installed.

### 5.5 · 09:55–10:15 · Google Docs — write once, together (20 min)
**Teach (8 min) · Practise (12 min)**

Teach:
- **Styles, not manual formatting.** Heading 1 / 2 / 3 → then *Insert → Table of contents* → then *View → Show outline*. Show the outline pane updating live. This one habit is worth the whole session.
- **Comments vs. Suggesting mode** — @-mention a colleague, assign a comment as a task, resolve it.
- **Version history** — *File → Version history → See version history*, name a version, restore. Demonstrate deliberately "ruining" the document and restoring it. This gets an audible reaction and permanently removes fear of experimenting.
- Export to `.docx` / PDF; email as attachment directly from Docs.

Practise — build the project brief:
- Create `Project Brief — Training Needs 2026` in `02 Reports`.
- Three headings using Heading 2: *Background*, *What we will ask*, *Who we will ask*. Two sentences under each.
- Insert a table of contents.
- Leave a comment on your buddy's brief asking one question; your buddy resolves it.
- Name the current version "v1 — before the survey".

### 5.6 · 10:15–10:35 · Google Sheets — answer a question with data (20 min)
**Teach (8 min) · Practise (12 min)**

Teach only what is needed for the scenario — resist teaching Sheets as a general spreadsheet course.
- Open the seeded response sheet, *File → Make a copy* into `01 Raw Data`.
- **Freeze row 1**, then *Data → Create a filter*.
- Four formulas, no more: `=SUM()`, `=AVERAGE()`, `=COUNTIF()`, `=COUNTA()`. Type them live, including one deliberate error, to show the error message and the correction.
- *Insert → Chart* → change chart type → move chart to its own sheet.

Practise:
- How many respondents are from each department? (`COUNTIF`)
- What is the average self-rated confidence score? (`AVERAGE`)
- Build a column chart of requests per training topic.
- Rename the tab. Colour the tab.

**Facilitator note:** if the room is struggling, drop `COUNTA` and the chart formatting rather than the filter — the filter is a daily-use skill, chart cosmetics are not.

### 5.7 · 10:35–10:45 · Consolidation and parking lot (10 min)
- **60-second pair quiz**, out loud, no slides: *Where does a Google Doc actually live? What is the difference between Commenter and Editor? What does version history protect you from?*
- Start a visible **parking lot** flipchart for off-topic questions. Promise to address it at 15:45. Then actually do.
- Announce exactly when Session 2 restarts, and ask everyone to leave laptops open and logged in over tea.

## 6. SESSION 2 — Making the apps work together
**11:05–12:45 · 100 minutes**

**Session goal:** stop using apps one at a time. Build one chain that runs itself.

### 6.1 · 11:05–11:15 · Recap and workflow thinking (10 min)
Draw this on the whiteboard and leave it up for the rest of the day:

```
COLLECT  →  STORE  →  ANALYSE  →  REPORT  →  SHARE
 Forms      Drive      Sheets      Docs      Gmail
                                  Slides    Calendar
```

Ask the room: *"For the weekly task you named this morning — which of those five steps do you currently do by hand?"* Two or three answers, no more. This converts the abstract diagram into their own work.

### 6.2 · 11:15–11:40 · Google Forms — build the survey (25 min)
**Teach (10 min) · Practise (15 min)**

Teach:
- Question types and when each is right: short answer, paragraph, multiple choice, checkboxes, dropdown, **linear scale** (perfect for confidence ratings), grid.
- **Required** toggle, and why over-using it kills response rates.
- **Sections** and simple branching (*Go to section based on answer*).
- Settings: collect email addresses, limit to one response, allow edit after submit, show progress bar.
- **Response validation** — number ranges, email format. Teach this: it is the difference between clean and dirty data.
- Themes, header image, and the *Send* options (link / email / embed / QR).

Practise — build "Staff Training Needs Survey 2026" with at least:
- Department (dropdown, required)
- Years of service (short answer, number validation 0–45)
- Confidence with digital tools (linear scale 1–5)
- Training topics wanted (checkboxes, with "Other")
- One open comment (paragraph)

### 6.3 · 11:40–11:50 · Live submission — the moment it clicks (10 min)
- Everyone submits **two responses** to your master form (one as themselves, one imaginary).
- Project the response summary and refresh it live. Watch the charts move as the room types.

This is the highest-engagement moment in Session 2. Do not rush past it — let people watch their own answer appear in the aggregate. Ten minutes here buys attention for the next thirty.

### 6.4 · 11:50–12:10 · Forms → Sheets, and analysis (20 min)
**Teach (8 min) · Practise (12 min)**

Teach:
- *Responses → Link to Sheets*. Explain clearly: this sheet is **live**. New submissions append automatically. Nobody re-types anything, ever again.
- Because it is live, never sort or delete rows in the response tab — **do analysis on a second tab**. Teach this rule firmly; it is the most common way beginners destroy their own data.
- **Filter views** vs filters — a filter view lets you slice the data without changing what others see.
- **Pivot tables** — *Insert → Pivot table*: rows = Department, values = COUNTA of respondent. Then add Confidence as an average. Pivot tables intimidate beginners for about ninety seconds and then delight them.

Practise:
- Link the form to a sheet.
- Build one pivot: training topic demand by department.
- Insert a chart from the pivot.

### 6.5 · 12:10–12:30 · Sheets → Docs → Slides (20 min)
**Teach (7 min) · Practise (13 min)**

Teach:
- Copy a chart in Sheets → paste into Docs → choose **"Link to spreadsheet"**. Change a number in Sheets, return to Docs, click **Update**. Do this live. It is the single most impressive non-AI thing you will show today.
- Same linked paste into Slides.
- Slides essentials, fast: use **Layouts** (not text boxes), *Slide → Apply layout*, theme, speaker notes, *Present*.

Practise — build the deliverable:
- Add findings and the linked chart to `Project Brief`, renaming it `Training Needs Report`.
- Build a **5-slide** deck: Title / What we asked / What we found (linked chart) / Recommendation / Next steps.
- Change one value in Sheets and update both the Doc and the deck.

### 6.6 · 12:30–12:40 · Share and circulate (10 min)
- Set folder permissions: `03 Final` shared with buddy as **Viewer**.
- From Docs: *File → Email → Email this file*.
- Google Calendar: create a "Training Needs Review" meeting, attach the Doc from Drive, add your buddy as a guest, add Google Meet, set a notification.
- Mention Gmail basics only as they serve the workflow: labels, filters, schedule send, confidential mode.

### 6.7 · 12:40–12:45 · Checkpoint (5 min)
On paper, each participant writes their **own** version of the COLLECT→SHARE chain for one real task from their job. Collect nothing — this is for them. Tell them Session 3 will rebuild it with AI.

## 7. SESSION 3 — Doing the same work with AI
**13:30–15:30 · 120 minutes**

**Session goal:** use Gemini and NotebookLM to compress the Session 2 workflow, and know exactly when *not* to.

**Post-lunch energy warning:** this session sits in the worst attention window of the day. It is built with a stand-up break at 14:30, frequent hands-on switching, and the emotional peak (§7.9) placed at 15:00. Do not front-load theory.

### 7.1 · 13:30–13:40 · Reset and framing (10 min)
Stand-up, 2-minute stretch. Then, in plain language:
- What a large language model actually does: it predicts likely text. It is a **language** engine, not a **truth** engine.
- **Hallucination** — it will invent a citation, a statistic or a regulation with total confidence. Show one live example if you can produce one safely.
- **The golden rule, repeated all session:** *AI drafts, you decide.* Never send anything you have not read.
- It is excellent at: summarising, restructuring, rephrasing, brainstorming, formatting, translating, first drafts. It is unreliable at: arithmetic on your data, current facts, anything it cannot see.

### 7.2 · 13:40–13:50 · Data safety — the non-negotiable block (10 min)
Your audience includes government-adjacent and NGO staff handling other people's personal data. This block is not optional.

**Never paste into a free consumer AI tool:**
- NIC numbers, passport numbers, addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth
- Beneficiary lists, patient or client records, staff disciplinary files
- Salary data tied to named individuals
- Unpublished tender documents, contracts, or anything commercially confidential
- Login credentials or API keys

**Safe practice to teach:**
- **De-identify before you paste.** Replace names with "Participant 1, 2, 3". Replace an NIC with "[ID]".
- Paste *aggregates and summaries*, not raw records. Your pivot table output is fine; the response sheet with names is not.
- Free-tier consumer terms differ from enterprise terms. Assume your organisation's policy is the binding one, and check it before you paste anything at work.
- Sri Lanka's **Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022** exists and the Data Protection Authority is established, though the substantive obligations have been brought into force in stages — treat compliance as a live obligation to check with your own organisation rather than something safely in the future.

Give the room a **one-line test** they will remember: *"Would I be comfortable if this text appeared in tomorrow's newspaper with my name on it?"* If no, don't paste it.

### 7.3 · 13:50–14:10 · Gemini — the basics that actually matter (20 min)
**Teach (10 min) · Practise (10 min)**

Tour gemini.google.com: prompt box, model selector, file upload, image upload, chat history, Canvas toggle, Gems panel.

Then teach the **only prompting framework beginners need — R-T-C-F**:

| | | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **R** — Role | Who should it be? | *You are an HR officer in a Sri Lankan NGO.* |
| **T** — Task | One clear verb | *Summarise the following survey comments into themes.* |
| **C** — Context | What it needs to know | *60 staff, mixed field and office roles, budget is limited.* |
| **F** — Format | Exactly what you want back | *A table with three columns: theme, how many mentioned it, suggested action. Maximum 6 rows.* |

Demonstrate the difference explicitly: run the bare prompt *"summarise this"*, then run the full R-T-C-F prompt on the same text. Put the two outputs side by side. This contrast teaches more than twenty minutes of explanation.

Practise — each participant writes one R-T-C-F prompt for a real task from their own job and runs it. Two volunteers read theirs aloud; improve them live with the room.

### 7.4 · 14:10–14:30 · Gemini on our own data + Canvas (20 min)
**Teach (7 min) · Practise (13 min)**

- Copy the **de-identified** pivot summary from Sheets and paste it into Gemini. Ask for the three most important findings and what a manager should do about each.
- Upload a file directly to Gemini (the fictional Annual Report PDF) and ask questions about it.
- Turn on **Canvas**. Ask it to draft the full training needs report. Then edit *inside* Canvas: select a paragraph, ask for shorter, more formal, add a risks section. Show **Export to Google Docs** — this is the bridge that replaces the missing Docs side panel.
- Ask Gemini to generate slide content, then paste it into your Slides deck.

Practise: draft your own report in Canvas from your own pivot summary, refine it twice, export to Docs.

**Note on free-tier context:** the free plan has a smaller context window (around 32k tokens) than paid plans. Practically, this means very long documents may need to be summarised in sections rather than pasted whole. Mention it so nobody is confused when a huge paste behaves oddly.

### 7.5 · 14:30–14:40 · Stand-up break + Gems (10 min)
Everyone stands. Then build one **Gem** together — a saved, reusable assistant:

> **Name:** Report Writer — [my organisation]
> **Instructions:** You write internal reports for a Sri Lankan NGO. Always use British English spelling. Always structure output as: Purpose, Findings, Recommendations, Next Steps. Keep to one page. Use plain language, no jargon. Never invent statistics — if a figure is missing, write [FIGURE NEEDED]. Ask me a clarifying question if the request is ambiguous.

Then run it. The "never invent statistics / write [FIGURE NEEDED]" instruction is a small piece of prompt engineering that visibly improves trustworthiness — point that out.

Tell them plainly: **this is the single highest-value thing they will build today**, because it turns a good prompt into a permanent tool.

### 7.6 · 14:40–15:00 · NotebookLM — grounded, cited answers (20 min)
**Teach (8 min) · Practise (12 min)**

Frame the difference clearly, because this is what beginners find confusing:

> **Gemini** answers from everything it knows — broad, creative, and capable of being wrong.
> **NotebookLM** answers *only* from the sources you give it, with a citation on every claim — narrow, grounded, and far harder to fool.

Teach:
- Create a notebook: `Training Needs 2026`.
- Add sources: the Docs report, the Annual Report PDF, a pasted text note, a website URL, a YouTube link. Up to **50 sources per notebook** on the free tier.
- Ask a question. Click a **citation number** and watch it jump to the exact sentence in the source. Do this slowly — it is the moment people trust the tool.
- Ask something the sources *don't* cover, and show it declining rather than inventing. This is the most important demonstration in the whole session.
- Studio outputs: **Mind Map** (10/day), **Audio Overview** (3/day), **Video Overview** (3/day), study guide, briefing doc, quizzes and flashcards.
- Notebooks now sync between NotebookLM and the Gemini app, so sources added in one appear in the other.

Practise:
- Build a notebook with at least three sources including your own report.
- Ask two questions and verify both answers against the citations.
- Generate one Mind Map.
- Start **one** Audio Overview per pair at around 14:55 (quotas are per user, but generation takes several minutes — start it now and listen during §7.8).

**Real use cases to name, matched to your audience:** a notebook of all circulars and policy documents; a notebook of every project report so you can ask "what did we say about water access in 2024?"; a notebook of a whole course's readings for the IT students in the room.

### 7.7 · 15:00–15:15 · THE RERUN — the payoff (15 min)
**Protect this block. Cut anything else first.**

Rebuild the Session 2 deliverable, AI-assisted, against the clock. Project a timer.

1. Paste the de-identified pivot summary into your **Report Writer Gem**.
2. Canvas → refine → **Export to Google Docs**.
3. Ask Gemini for a 5-slide outline → paste into Slides.
4. Paste the report into NotebookLM → ask it to check whether every recommendation is supported by the findings.

Then run the debrief — and give this the last five minutes, not thirty seconds:

- *Session 2 took about 50 minutes. That took about 8. What exactly did we save?*
- **Where did AI get it wrong?** Ask the room directly. Somebody's output will have invented a number, softened a finding, or used generic filler. Find it and display it. **An AI workshop that shows no failures has taught nothing.**
- *What still needed a human?* — the judgement, the local context, the decision, the accountability.
- Land the rule one final time: **AI drafts, you decide.**

### 7.8 · 15:15–15:30 · Wider view and honest limits (15 min)
Fast, demonstration-only, no hands-on:
- **Deep Research** in Gemini — multi-source research reports (free tier included).
- **Google Vids** — free on personal accounts with 50 AI credits/month; "Help me create" costs 5 credits, AI voiceover 1 credit. Good for quick internal training videos.
- Gemini **Connected Apps** for Gmail, Drive and Calendar — mention availability varies by account and region.
- Play the Audio Overviews started in §7.6.

**Then the honest limits slide** — this is what separates a credible trainer from a hype merchant:
- Free tiers have daily and weekly caps that reset; heavy use will hit them.
- Free-tier context windows are smaller — very long documents need chunking.
- Output quality varies by language; Sinhala and Tamil output is usable but weaker than English. **Advise: prompt in English, translate after, and always have a human check the translation** before it goes to a beneficiary or the public.
- Features and limits change month to month. Nothing shown today is permanent.
- **AI does not remove accountability.** If a report with your name on it contains an invented statistic, that is your mistake, not the tool's.

## 8. Wrap-up
**15:45–16:00 · 15 minutes**

- Clear the **parking lot** flipchart. Answer or honestly defer every item on it.
- **Three commitments**: each participant writes down one Google apps habit, one workflow to automate, and one AI use case they will apply within 30 days. Have them write it in a Doc in their own Drive, not on paper — it makes the point about where things live.
- **30-day path**: Week 1 — move one recurring task into Drive with proper sharing. Week 2 — build one Form-to-Sheets workflow. Week 3 — build one Gem for a task you repeat. Week 4 — build one NotebookLM notebook for a document set you search often.
- **Resources handout** — links to Google Workspace Learning Center, gemini.google.com, notebooklm.google.com, and your own follow-up contact.
- Feedback form — a Google Form, obviously. Project the live responses as they arrive. It is the perfect closing demonstration.
- Certificates.

### Suggested certificate criterion
Since a certificate is awarded, tie it to evidence rather than attendance. A participant qualifies if their Drive contains, by 15:30: **(a)** a shared folder, **(b)** a Form linked to a Sheet, **(c)** a report Doc containing a chart, and **(d)** one Gem or one NotebookLM notebook. Buddies verify each other in two minutes and report to you. It costs almost nothing and materially raises how hard people work in the hands-on blocks.

## 9. Bilingual delivery notes (Sinhala & English)

**The rule:** explain **concepts** in Sinhala, keep every **interface label** in English, always. The menus on their screens will say "Share", "Version history", "Pivot table". If you translate those, participants cannot find them afterwards. Say the English label, then explain what it does in Sinhala.

Practical technique — the **sandwich**:
1. English term: *"Version history"*
2. Sinhala explanation of what it does and why it matters
3. English term again while pointing at it on screen

Other guidance:
- Write the English term on the whiteboard when you first use it. Leave it up.
- Slides in English; your speech bilingual. Bilingual slides double the text and halve legibility.
- Handout in English with a short Sinhala glossary column — people search for English terms later.
- Check comprehension in Sinhala, but ask participants to *show you the screen* rather than say yes. In a mixed-seniority Sri Lankan room, people will say they understood when they have not. Screens do not lie.
- Do not code-switch mid-sentence when giving a click-by-click instruction. Finish the instruction in one language.

**Glossary for the handout** (adjust to the usage your audience is accustomed to — regional and institutional usage varies):

## 10. Handling a mixed-ability room

Your audience mixes senior professionals, NGO field staff and IT students. That spread is the biggest delivery risk of the day.

- **Buddy pairs** (§5.2), deliberately mixing an IT student with a less confident participant. Tell the students explicitly that helping is part of their learning — it gives them a role and stops them racing ahead in boredom.
- **Extension tasks** for fast finishers, written on the whiteboard so you never have to improvise:
  - Add conditional formatting to your Sheet
  - Add branching logic to your Form
  - Set up a Gmail filter that labels and archives automatically
  - Explore Apps Script (*Extensions → Apps Script*) — mention only, do not teach
  - Build a second Gem for a completely different task
- **Floor rule**: after you finish demonstrating, walk the room for the entire practice block. Never sit at the podium during hands-on time. Look at screens rather than waiting for hands.
- **Never leave a participant behind on a chained task.** If someone cannot complete §6.2, they cannot start §6.4. Keep a copy of the finished artefact ready to share so a stuck participant can copy it and rejoin the chain.

## 11. Risk register and fallbacks

| Risk | Likelihood | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi fails or saturates with 30 devices | **High** | Mobile hotspot ready; switch to projector-only demo; distribute pre-recorded screen captures; do the paper workflow-mapping exercise (§6.7) while it recovers |
| Participants can't remember their Google password | **High** | Caught at gate check (§2); if not, pair them onto a buddy's screen for the day and give them the exercise files afterwards |
| Someone expects Gemini inside Docs and it isn't there | **High** | Explained upfront (§0); show the Canvas → Export to Docs bridge instead |
| Nobody submits to the master Form so charts look empty | Medium | Seeded 60-response sheet prepared at T-1 |
| Gemini or NotebookLM is slow or rate-limited | Medium | Have a pre-generated notebook and a pre-generated Audio Overview saved locally |
| Session 1 or 2 runs over | **High** | Cut §5.7 quiz, §6.6 Calendar, §7.8 wider view — in that order. Never cut §7.7 |
| Room is far more advanced than expected | Low | Extension tasks (§10); go deeper on pivot tables, Gems and Apps Script |
| Room is far less confident than expected | Medium | Drop the pivot table entirely; drop Slides to a 5-minute demo; keep Forms, Sheets basics, Gemini and NotebookLM |

## 12. Prompt bank

Hand these out. Beginners need working examples far more than they need theory.

**Summarising**
> You are an administrative officer at a Sri Lankan NGO. Summarise the survey comments below into a maximum of five themes. For each theme give: the theme name, roughly how many comments mentioned it, and one suggested action. Return it as a table. Do not add themes that are not present in the text.

**Writing**
> You are writing an internal memo to a Director. Purpose: request approval for a digital skills training programme. Context: 60 staff, survey shows 70% rate their digital confidence at 3 or below out of 5, budget is limited. Format: under 300 words, structured as Purpose / Evidence / Request / Cost implication. Formal but plain language.

**Improving a draft**
> Here is my draft. Rewrite it to be clearer and about 30% shorter. Keep every factual claim exactly as written — do not add, remove or change any figure. Tell me at the end which sentences you found unclear and why.

**Data**
> The table below is a summary of survey responses. Identify the three most important findings for a manager, and for each explain why it matters in one sentence. If the data is insufficient to support a conclusion, say so rather than guessing.

**Planning**
> Help me plan a two-hour internal training session on Google Forms for 15 staff with low digital confidence. Give me a minute-by-minute run sheet, a list of what I need to prepare beforehand, and the three most likely things to go wrong.

**Translating (always verify)**
> Translate the following notice into Sinhala. Use formal register suitable for an official circular. Keep all technical product names in English. After the translation, list any phrases where you were uncertain about the best rendering.

**NotebookLM questions**
> What do these documents say about staff training needs? Cite the source for each point.
> Where do these two documents disagree with each other?
> What questions does a decision-maker still need answered that these sources do not cover?

**A prompt to teach caution**
> What is the current government policy on X?

Run this one live and let the room examine the answer critically. It is a reliable way to surface confident, unverifiable output — which is exactly the lesson.

## 14. What I changed from your original outline, and why

Your three-session structure was sound. These are the adjustments that matter most:

1. **Session 3 assumed Gemini inside Docs and Sheets.** On free personal Gmail accounts that side panel does not exist. The plan now routes everything through gemini.google.com + Canvas + export, which is free and works for everyone in the room. **This is the change most likely to save your session tomorrow.**

2. **AI moved into the first 30 minutes.** A 10-minute demonstration at 09:25 hooks the room during peak attention and stops anyone leaving at lunch feeling they haven't seen what the poster advertised. The deep AI work still lives in Session 3.

3. **One scenario replaces a feature tour.** Adult learners retain workflows and forget menus. Every block now produces one piece of a single deliverable, so each skill has an obvious purpose at the moment it is taught.

4. **Your "redo the workflow with AI" idea became the emotional centre of the day** and got a protected 15-minute slot with a visible timer and a side-by-side comparison. It was the strongest idea in your outline — it deserved more than an afterthought at the end.

5. **Added a data-safety block before any hands-on AI.** Your audience handles other people's personal data. Teaching them to use AI without teaching them what not to paste into it would be a genuine professional risk.

6. **Added a deliberate "where did AI get it wrong" segment.** Showing only successes produces overconfident users, which is worse than no training at all.

7. **Added a 30-minute technical gate check before 09:00.** Login and connectivity problems are the top cause of overrun in beginner Google workshops. Solving them during registration protects your teaching time.

8. **Tied the certificate to four produced artefacts** rather than attendance. It sharpens effort during hands-on blocks at almost no administrative cost.

9. **Added explicit fallbacks and a cut-order** so that when you run late — and you will — you already know what to drop, and you drop the right things.

10. **Fix "Bard" on the poster and slides.** Bard became Gemini. On an AI course, this is the detail the sharpest person in the room will notice.

Information Processing and Management 63 (2026) 104466, Hae Sun Jung of Sungkyunkwan University and Haein Lee of Dongguk University-Seoul, received July 2025 and online 5 November 2025. A zero-shot GPT-4o multi-agent framework for daily Bitcoin trading, with three domain agents reading technical indicators, on-chain metrics and macro-financial variables, each emitting a natural-language summary rather than a number, and a meta trading agent that synthesises those summaries into one of BUY, SELL or HOLD together with a written rationale. Two further text agents, one over Reddit and one over news wires, are added in ablation. Everything is backtested against LSTM, GRU, DLinear and PatchTST across three input windows over a common 268-day test period under a shared execution model, and there is no fine-tuning and no gradient training anywhere in the agent path.

This is the closest published system to the orchestration structure I am proposing, and it is the most useful paper in my set precisely because the orchestration is the part it does not do. The agents are genuinely heterogeneous in modality, technical, on-chain, macro, social and news, which is the property I have been arguing separates my design from [[MacroHFT, Memory Augmented Context-aware Reinforcement Learning On High Frequency Trading]]'s six architecturally identical DDQN sub-agents, but the meta-agent's relationship to them is a fixed prompt template, so every agent's influence is constant regardless of whether it is saying anything useful that day. The ablation then shows exactly what that costs, <font color="#ffc000">adding a news agent takes total return from 21.75 % to 11.39 %</font>, and nothing in the architecture can notice or respond. That is the cleanest quantified case for my main gap that I have found, and the fact that the authors read it as a signal-integration problem rather than an orchestration problem is the opening.

> [!NOTE] G-EVAL as used here
> G-EVAL (Liu et al., 2023) is a protocol where an LLM scores generated text against a rubric in a zero-shot prompt, rather than against a reference. Jung and Lee apply it to *pairs* of agent outputs, scoring each daily Reddit summary against the matching news summary on two rubrics, a **conflict score** for semantic divergence in tone, outlook and implication, and an **actionability score** for whether the combination yields a clear and timely trading signal. Both are 0 to 1. The scoring model is GPT-4o, which is also the model that wrote the summaries being scored.

#### Gap it's addressing

- **LLM work in crypto has stayed at the level of isolated tasks rather than end-to-end decisions.** LLMs get used for sentiment classification, entity recognition or news summarisation and the output is fed onward as a feature, so the language model never holds the decision. Where end-to-end LLM traders do exist, the paper charges them with univariate price inputs, short prediction windows and fine-tuning that does not generalise, which fits the Kumar and Makri work it cites but not [[FinMe, A Performance-Enhanced LLM Trading Agent With Layered Memory and Character Design]] or [[FinCon, A Synthesized LLM Multi-Agent System with Conceptual Verbal Reinforcement for Enhanced Financial Decision Making]], neither of which is engaged with here.
- **Sentiment enters prior pipelines as a static scalar and cannot participate in inference.** FinBERT polarity scores and VADER-style lexicon outputs are auxiliary features appended to a price model, so a sentiment reading of 0.7 means the same thing in every market state and carries no reasoning that could be inspected or contradicted. This is the gap the whole design answers, and it is the one that is genuinely under-addressed rather than merely under-cited.
- **Backtesting setups in this literature are loosely specified and mutually incomparable.** Capital base, execution timing, fee assumptions and trade frequency vary or go unreported, which makes cross-paper performance claims unreliable. The response is a single execution harness that every strategy runs inside, which is the methodological contribution I care about most and the one that survives even if the headline result does not.

The third point is the one the paper actually delivers on. The first two are correctly identified but the framework addresses them through prompt engineering rather than through any structural mechanism, so the gap and the fix are not the same size.

#### Data and setup

Structured data spans 1 March 2021 to 28 February 2025, 1,461 aligned daily records after an inner join on date. Market data is Binance OHLCV pulled through `ccxt`, chosen for liquidity and representativeness. On-chain data is five fields from Bitinfocharts covering confirmation time, hash rate, transaction fees and active addresses, selected on predictive relevance established in the authors' own prior work. Macro-financial data is five Yahoo Finance series, the dollar index, gold, the S&P 500, the VIX and WTI crude, forward-filled from Friday close through the weekend so that <font color="#ffc000">1,007 raw trading days are imputed up to 1,461 crypto-calendar days</font>, which means roughly 30 % of the macro channel is a repeated Friday value.

Technical indicators are computed in Python across three groups, trend following with SMA and EMA over 3 to 120-day windows plus MACD, momentum with RSI, raw momentum and a 10-day rate of change, and oscillators with Stochastic RSI, Stochastic and a 14-day Williams %R. The 120-day window costs the first 120 rows, so the effective analysis sample is 1,341 days and every reported result rests on that.

Textual data is 67,075 Reddit posts from crypto subreddits via Reddit Archive and 8,657 Bitcoin news articles from LexisNexis, but covering only 304 trading days from 1 May 2024 to 28 February 2025. Text is never merged into the structured join, it is aligned by date at the prompt-construction stage only, and it is capped at 90,000 words per three-day decision window with a 30,000-word daily limit per source, with stratified sampling in proportion to source counts when the cap binds. Figure 7 shows only a handful of days exceeded the Lexis cap, so the sampling rarely fires.

The chronological split is 64:16:20 with MinMaxScaler fitted on training only, and the test period is 6 June 2024 to 28 February 2025, 268 days, held common across all three window sizes. Baselines get a full sweep over hidden dimensions of 32, 64 and 128, learning rates of 0.0005 (0.01 to 0.05 for DLinear), batch size 64, Adam, up to 100 epochs with early stopping at patience 10, and a grid search over trading thresholds. Crucially, <font color="#ffff00">baseline thresholds were calibrated so that average trade counts land in the 70 to 110 range</font>, matching the trading frequency the LLM agent produces on its own, which removes the obvious confound of one strategy winning by trading more. All agents run GPT-4o at temperature 0.7, top-p 1.0 and 1,024 max tokens, and every configuration is repeated across 20 seeds with results averaged and dispersion reported.

Execution is $100,000 initial capital, one trade per day maximum, no leverage, a 0.1 % transaction fee and 0.1 % slippage applied on both buys and sells.

#### Model architecture and training

![[Pasted image 20260923120001.png]]

The three domain agents each read a sliding window of 3, 14 or 30 days over their own modality and produce a prose summary of recent conditions, explicitly without making a price prediction, one on momentum dynamics and indicator signals, one on network activity and participation shifts, one on global risk sentiment and volatility spillovers. The meta trading agent receives all summaries and selects an action with a rationale. Two constraints do the hallucination control, agents run from fixed domain-specific templates rather than open-ended generation, and the meta-agent is instructed to synthesise without altering factual content or introducing information the upstream agents did not supply. GPT-4o was selected over Claude, Gemini and FinGPT on instruction-hierarchy evaluations, which matters for a design where conflicting agent outputs have to be aggregated under a single controlling instruction.

The baselines are point forecasters, so a threshold rule converts a predicted close into an action, and this is the mechanism that makes the two strategy families comparable at all:

$$\Delta p_t = \frac{\hat{y}_t - y_{t-1}}{y_{t-1}}, \qquad a_t = \begin{cases} \text{BUY}, & \Delta p_t > \theta \\ \text{SELL}, & \Delta p_t < -\theta \\ \text{HOLD}, & |\Delta p_t| \le \theta \end{cases}$$

where $\theta = 0.015$ for the 3 and 14-day windows and $0.010$ for the 30-day window, the wider band on short horizons reflecting higher noise. Working in relative change rather than absolute level is the right call, since a directional signal is more robust than a point forecast, and the dead band is the only thing stopping the baselines from churning.

Timing is strict and identical across strategies. Signals use information up to and including the close of day $t-1$, execution happens at the open of day $t$ with fees and slippage applied, and the portfolio is marked at the close of day $t$, so nothing from day $t$ is visible at decision time. Alongside the usual profitability and risk-adjusted metrics the paper reports three trading-behaviour diagnostics, exposure time, average holding period and turnover, the last defined as

$$\text{Turnover} = \frac{1}{C_0}\sum_{t=1}^{T} |\Delta q_t| \cdot P_t$$

where $\Delta q_t$ is the change in position on day $t$, $P_t$ the contemporaneous price and $C_0$ the initial capital, so a turnover of 7.60 means 7.6 times the starting capital was traded across the test period. This is the reporting discipline I want to copy, because it lets the authors answer the "did it just trade more" objection with evidence instead of assertion.

#### Findings

- **The framework wins at the three-day window, and the margin sits inside its own dispersion.** Total return of 21.75 % ± 3.03 against LSTM's 20.05 % ± 3.74 and buy-and-hold's 18.90 %, with a Sharpe of 1.08 ± 0.11 against LSTM's 1.05 ± 0.17 and buy-and-hold's 0.80. The abstract reports the Sharpe advantage as 0.003 where Table 3 gives 1.08 against 1.05, and either way the gap is a small fraction of a standard deviation that spans it. Against the passive benchmark the result is more convincing, since the MDD falls from 22.52 % to 14.69 % ± 0.87 and Calmar rises from 1.19 to 1.99 ± 0.25, but <font color="#ffc000">LSTM still holds both the best drawdown at 13.10 % and the best Calmar at 2.07</font> in the same table, which the paper does not dwell on. I would report this as competitive with the strongest recurrent baseline on this sample, not as surpassing it.
- **The advantage reverses as the horizon lengthens, and the paper is upfront about this.** The table below is the one I would put in front of a supervisor, because the story is not that LLM reasoning beats time-series models, it is that it beats them on short horizons and loses on long ones.

| Window | Best total return | Best Sharpe | GPT-4o agent (return / Sharpe / MDD) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-day | Ours 21.75 % | Ours 1.08 | 21.75 % / 1.08 / 14.69 % |
| 14-day | GRU 21.65 % | PatchTST 1.15 | 20.34 % / 1.02 / 15.88 % |
| 30-day | GRU 22.14 % | GRU 1.55 | 17.40 % / 0.77 / 20.49 % |

  At 30 days the agent's Sharpe of 0.77 ± 0.22 is below buy-and-hold's 0.80 and its Calmar of 1.14 ± 0.33 is barely above the passive 1.19, so on this sample the zero-shot agent stops earning its complexity once the window is long enough for a recurrent model to exploit. The authors attribute this to the absence of memory consolidation or temporal smoothing, which is consistent with the pattern but is an explanation rather than a test.

- **What the framework actually buys is the drawdown profile rather than the return.** Cumulative return tracks buy-and-hold closely over the test period while the drawdown path is materially shallower, around 15 % against 20 % at the worst point, and the smoother trajectory is what drives the Calmar and Sortino gaps rather than any headline return advantage.

  ![[Pasted image 20260923120003.png]]

  The behaviour diagnostics support reading this as a property of the decisions rather than of trading intensity, since the agent runs 83.67 ± 11.22 trades against GRU's 74.35 and LSTM's 90.12, turnover of 7.60 ± 1.12 against 6.90 and 6.75, exposure time of 93.15 % and an average holding period of 22 days against 18.35 and 19.02. Nothing there suggests the gain came from churning, though I note that 84 trades over 268 days sits awkwardly with a 22-day mean holding period, and the paper does not reconcile the two definitions.
- **Reddit sentiment helps, news hurts, and the two together are worse than Reddit alone, which is the most interesting result in the paper.**

  ![[Pasted image 20260923120002.png]]

  Against the 21.75 % structured-only baseline, adding the news agent drops total return to 11.39 % ± 1.06 with Sharpe collapsing from 1.08 to 0.55, adding the social media agent lifts it to 23.30 % ± 2.41 with Sharpe 1.12 ± 0.12 and Calmar 2.01, and adding both lands at 18.98 % ± 5.74, below the baseline that used neither. Scoped honestly, the social gain of 1.55 points sits well inside a ± 2.41 band and I would not claim it, whereas <font color="#ffc000">the news loss of 10.36 points is far outside any dispersion in the table</font> and is the finding worth carrying. The authors read this as community sentiment being more tightly coupled to short-horizon retail behaviour than formal news is, which is plausible on this asset and this window, and untested elsewhere.
- **The G-EVAL diagnostics show frequent semantic clash between the two text sources and only moderate actionability even without it.** Conflict scores average 0.42 with a median of 0.30 and a top quartile above 0.60, and the distribution is visibly bimodal, a large mass of low-conflict days and a secondary cluster near 0.7. Actionability averages 0.55 with a median of 0.60 and a maximum of 0.90.

  ![[Pasted image 20260923120004.png]]

  The authors draw two conclusions, that divergence between sources makes a coherent reasoning chain harder to build, and that even agreeing sources often fail to produce a usable signal. The first is consistent with the dual-agent degradation, the second is the more uncomfortable one and is the reason I would treat actionability as the more informative of the two scores.

#### Limitations

- **The headline advantage does not clear its own error bars and no significance test is run anywhere.** Repeating 20 seeds and reporting dispersion is better practice than [[FinHEAR, Human Expertise and Adaptive Risk-Aware Temporal Reasoning for Financial Decision-Making]] or FinMem manage, and it deserves credit, but seed dispersion measures LLM sampling variance and not variation across market paths, and there is still one asset, one 268-day test window and one regime, a period over which passive Bitcoin returned 18.90 %. A 1.70 point edge over LSTM with standard deviations of 3.03 and 3.74 is not an edge I would defend without a paired test, and the paper offers none.
- **Friction is a flat constant on the deepest book in crypto, and the sensitivity band is too narrow to bind.** Total return moves from 22.03 % to 21.61 % as fees and slippage go from 0.05 % to 0.15 %, which the authors read as robustness. Taken against a reported turnover of 7.60, a 0.2 % swing in round-trip cost should cost something closer to 1.5 points of capital than the 0.42 observed, and the paper does not reconcile the two, so I would treat the sensitivity analysis as under-specified rather than as evidence that costs do not matter. There is also a small internal inconsistency, since Table 7 reports 21.82 % at the 0.10 % setting where Table 3 reports 21.75 % for what should be the same configuration. <font color="#ff0000">There is no market impact term anywhere</font>, which for Bitcoin at $100k of capital is defensible and for anything thinner is not.
- **Threshold and hyperparameter selection is described as a grid search without ever naming the selection set.** The paper states an optimal configuration per window, hidden dimension 32 and threshold 0.015 at three days, 64 and 0.015 at fourteen, 128 and 0.010 at thirty, and separately says thresholds were calibrated so trade counts fell in the 70 to 110 band. Both are reasonable procedures, but the validation split exists and is never mentioned as the scoring set for either, and since the threshold is the only thing converting a baseline forecast into a strategy, any leakage there lands directly on the comparison the paper's central claim rests on.
- **The text-agent ablation confounds content with prompt budget and runs at one window size only.** Adding an agent adds thousands of words to the prompt as well as a new information source, and there is no placebo agent, no prompt-length control and no run with news content shuffled across dates, so the 10-point news degradation is consistent with semantic conflict, with dilution of the structured summaries in a longer context, or with both. The ablation is also confined to the three-day setting because running it across all windows was impractical, which means the one configuration where the agent is strongest is the only one where the text contribution is measured.
- **The interpretability evaluation is the same model family grading its own output, and is never checked against outcomes.** GPT-4o writes the Reddit and news summaries and GPT-4o scores their conflict and actionability, so the scores measure internal consistency of one model's reading rather than anything external. The authors say plainly in the limitations that they did not investigate whether conflict and actionability correlate with subsequent realised returns or error rates, and until that is done the scores diagnose the reasoning process without establishing that the process is tracking anything.
- **Zero-shot with fixed templates is the design's cost as well as its selling point.** With no memory, no retrieval and no adaptation, each decision is reconstructed from the current window, which is the authors' own explanation for the 30-day degradation. They also concede sensitivity to prompt phrasing and possible degradation under distributional shift, and the template constraint that suppresses hallucination is the same constraint that limits reasoning flexibility, a trade-off the paper names but does not measure.

#### Why this matters for my project

- **This is the third documented case of a fixed-influence component degrading a multi-agent system, and the first that puts a number on it.** [[A Multimodal Foundation Agent for Financial Trading, Tool-Augmented, Diversified, and Generalist]] has auxiliary strategies returning 16 % on ETHUSD against a 29 % passive benchmark, [[AlphaAgents, Large Language Model Based Multi-Agents for Equity Portfolio Constructions]] drops its sentiment agent outright for insufficient coverage, and here a news agent costs 10.36 points of total return while the architecture has no way to notice. The three together are the evidentiary base for the main gap, and this one is the strongest because the degradation is measured under a controlled ablation rather than inferred. The obvious counterfactual, a system that could down-weight the news agent on days when it diverges, is exactly what I am proposing and exactly what nobody here builds.
- **Conflict and actionability are the first concrete answer I have seen to the comparable-confidence problem.** My orchestration function needs confidence on a common scale across an LLM analyst, an econometric estimator and an RL policy, and nothing in the literature supplies one, because each component's native confidence means something different. The move here is to stop asking the agents and score the *relationship between their outputs* externally on a fixed rubric, which sidesteps internal calibration entirely and generalises to any pair of producers that can emit text. I would take the mechanism and not the validation, since scoring pairwise conflict is cheap and establishing that it predicts anything is the work this paper skips.
- **That gives me a pre-registered test rather than an assumption, which is what the weight-entropy check already does for the routing claim.** The test is direct, compute conflict scores across agent pairs on CSE data, then check whether they correlate with realised next-period error or return before any of it touches the orchestrator. If conflict carries no signal, the conditioning set falls back to market state alone and I report that, and if it does, I have an information-state input that no prior system in my set uses. Either outcome is publishable and the cost is one scoring pass.
- **Another paper where execution friction never binds, which keeps the thin-market case open.** A flat 0.1 % fee and 0.1 % slippage on Binance, no impact term and a sensitivity band of ± 0.05 %, sitting alongside MacroHFT's 0.02 % flat and FinHEAR's single 5 bps check. The CSE charges materially more, the book is thin enough that impact is a separate cost on top of the spread, and every one of these systems was designed and tuned in a regime where cost is a rounding error. That is the sub-gap in one sentence, and this paper is a clean citation for it because its cost model is stated explicitly enough to be criticised.
- **Sentiment is out of scope for my project as of this note, so the news half of the ablation is the part that still speaks to my design.** My text channel is structure-aware retrieval over CSE annual reports, quarterlies and announcements rather than any sentiment series, and of the two text agents tested here the news agent is the nearer analogue, dated factual material summarised for a decision rather than community tone. That is the agent that cost 10.36 points of total return. The lesson is not that filings will hurt, since the information content is different and considerably higher, it is that <font color="#ffc000">a text channel bolted onto a working structured pipeline has to be shown to earn its weight rather than assumed to</font>, and that the demonstration costs one on-off ablation. Running that ablation on the retrieval agent is now a required experiment in my design rather than an optional one.
- **The horizon reversal is a prior against an all-LLM design, and it argues for my heterogeneity claim from the other direction.** GRU takes the 30-day window on return, Sharpe, Sortino and Calmar while the agent falls below passive on risk-adjusted terms, which says the cheap recurrent model is not a strawman baseline to be cleared but a component with a horizon where it genuinely wins. An orchestrator that can shift weight toward a recurrent or econometric component at long horizons and toward the reasoning agents at short ones is a concrete, testable instantiation of the main gap, and this paper supplies the horizon evidence for free.
- **The evaluation harness is the part I should copy wholesale, including the trade-count calibration.** Equalising baseline trade counts into the agent's 70 to 110 band before comparing returns removes the intensity confound by construction, and the diagnostics block of exposure time, average holding period and turnover is what lets the comparison be defended afterwards. Running my CSE comparison inside one harness with those three diagnostics reported is cheap and closes an objection before it is raised.
- **The discipline lesson runs the opposite way to the one intended, and I should take it anyway.** Reporting 20 seeds with dispersion is right, and the consequence is that the headline margin stops clearing its own error bars, which is visible only because they reported it. For my own work that means committing to dispersion and a paired significance test before I see the numbers, and accepting in advance that a result inside the bands gets written up as competitive rather than superior. <font color="#ffff00">An honest null on the orchestration weights is a better outcome than a 1.7-point win I cannot defend</font>, and this paper is the example of what the alternative looks like.

2021 5th SLAAI International Conference on Artificial Intelligence, Nanayakkara, Wanniarachchi and Vidanagama out of the Department of Information Technology at General Sir John Kotelawala Defence University, Rathmalane. A web and mobile portfolio management platform for the Colombo Stock Exchange with a price prediction module attached, a stacked LSTM for short and mid term horizons and a geometric Brownian motion model for the long term, with requirements elicited through interviews with CSE officials and a 17 question survey of 233 investors, deployed on Azure. DOI 10.1109/SLAAI-ICAI54477.2021.9664735.

This is the closest thing in my set to a direct predecessor, it is the only paper I have that builds a CSE-facing portfolio system rather than a model, and it is worth reading for the requirements and the institutional access rather than for the modelling, because the models are off the shelf and, more to the point, <font color="#ffc000">not one of them is ever evaluated on a Colombo-listed stock</font>. The forecasting evidence in a paper titled for the CSE comes from Apple, Microsoft and Nike. So I treat this as prior art establishing that the platform gap is real and locally recognised, and as a worked example of what happens when an evaluation protocol is never fixed before the build starts.

> [!important]
> **CSE provided an official XML API for this project.** The paper states the CSE data endpoint was "officially provided by CSE to fulfill the current development purposes", alongside two JSON endpoints for international exchanges. That is the most useful sentence in the paper for me, because my own data access question is still open and this is evidence that a written request from a university project has been granted before. The interviews name the roles to approach, head of R&D, head of Public Relations, and the Chief Information Officer.

#### Gap it's addressing

- **CSE offers no official portfolio management platform with prediction capability.** The paper walks through what an investor has to do manually instead, revaluing holdings, re-applying a sector-based selection rule across health care, leisure, business and so on, summing invested capital, computing expected returns against realised returns, and deciding what to drop, all through a broker or a brokerage portal rather than a first-party tool.
- **The intermediation itself is the risk being argued about.** Brokerage advisors both advise and transact, and the paper cites the Securities and Exchange Commission filing cases against four long-established broking firms, with the 2011 Radiant Gems International PLC share price manipulation as the named incident. The framing is that opacity between investor and market is a credibility problem for the exchange, not only an inconvenience.
- **Existing purchasing platforms let investors transact but not analyse.** Both local and international platforms cover buy and sell without portfolio-level analysis or forecasting, so the paper positions the contribution as the combination, management plus prediction in one investor-facing system, rather than either piece on its own.

#### Data and setup

There are two distinct data collections here and they answer different questions. The requirements side is the better executed of the two.

- **Requirements.** Semi-structured interviews with CSE officials including the head of R&D, the head of Public Relations and the CIO, covering investor behaviour, legal factors and non-functional requirements. Then a questionnaire of 17 questions covering functional requirements, non-functional requirements, visual design, UI/UX and content management, put to a sample of 233 investors drawn across professions, doctors, engineers, judges, businessmen, teachers, accountants and architects, with responses put through statistical analysis before the requirement set was fixed.
- **Market data for the long-term model.** Pulled through the Pandas Yahoo Finance data reader, with the window set programmatically as `current.year-3` to `datetime.now()`, so a rolling three years of history, and <font color="#ffc000">Adjusted Close</font> as the single input series.
- **Market data for the short and mid term model.** Downloaded as CSV from the Tiingo API, 2016 to present, fourteen columns available including symbol, date, OHLCV, the adjusted series, `divCash` and `splitFactor`, of which only `close` is used to train.

Neither source carries Colombo-listed equities, which is the structural problem with the evaluation and which I come back to below.

#### System and models

Frontend in HTML, Materialize CSS, Bootstrap and React.js, backend in PHP, MySQL for storage, four API endpoints, and the whole application hosted on Microsoft Azure across IaaS and PaaS on a virtual machine, with both models trained and deployed through Azure Machine Learning Studio to keep model processing off the request path.

Prediction is split by horizon, which is the one genuinely reusable design decision in the build. Short term is defined as 1 to 7 days and mid term as 15 to 30 days, both served by a <font color="#ffff00">stacked LSTM</font>, and anything longer is served by <font color="#ffff00">geometric Brownian motion</font>. The reasoning given is that LSTM is strong on sequence-based prediction over past information while a stochastic process is the appropriate tool once the horizon is long enough that the sequence signal has decayed.

The Brownian motion path is described in words only, so the formula below is the standard construction the described steps amount to, not an equation the paper prints. After the series is normalised with a logarithm, the paper computes mean, standard deviation, variance, volatility and drift, then simulates forward at a 95% confidence level:

$$\mu = \mathbb{E}[r_t], \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(r_t)}, \qquad r_t = \ln\frac{S_t}{S_{t-1}}$$

$$S_{t+\Delta t} = S_t \exp\left[\left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)\Delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\; Z\right], \qquad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

where:
- $r_t$ is the log return, which is the series the moments are estimated on after the logarithmic normalisation step
- $\mu - \sigma^2/2$ is the drift the paper reports calculating separately from volatility, the Itô correction being the reason drift and mean are not the same number
- $Z$ is the random shock redrawn per simulated path, so the output is a fan of trajectories whose central tendency is plotted as the prediction and whose spread gives the 95% envelope

The LSTM side is specified only at the level of library calls. `MinMaxScaler` from scikit-learn for feature scaling, NumPy reshaping into a 3D array, a Keras `Sequential` model with `Dense` and `LSTM` layers, `adam` as optimizer, `mean_squared_error` as loss, 100 epochs at batch size 64 with verbose 1, then `inverse_transform` back to price space and Matplotlib for display. <font color="#ff0000">Layer count, hidden units, lookback window length and the train/test split dates are all absent</font>, so the model as described cannot be reproduced.

#### Findings

- **All twelve functional test cases pass, at self-assigned scores between 92% and 100%.** Testing was manual plus automation through Selenium and Katalon Studio, conducted on a system the paper describes as 95% complete, with user acceptance testing by the author and by senior CSE officials. The lowest score is email and SMS profit and loss notification at 92%, the two perfect scores are adding and removing stocks from a portfolio and candlestick analysis of a single share.

  ![[Pasted image 20260919234003.png]]

- **The only quantitative forecasting evidence in the paper is three percentage differences from the Brownian motion model, and they range widely.** Microsoft comes in at 4.88% difference between original and predicted price, Nike at 12.1% and Apple at 18.51%, from which the paper concludes "more than 80% level of accuracy" in the results section, while the abstract and the opening of the same section claim more than 85% accuracy for both models. <font color="#ffc000">A spread of 4.88% to 18.51% across three large-cap US stocks is the honest summary</font>, and the aggregate accuracy figure is doing work the three data points do not support.
- **The Apple figure shows the mechanism clearly, and it also shows the model tracking below the realised series for most of the year.** The green predicted path is the smooth central trajectory of the simulated fan while the red realised path sits above it from roughly mid 2020 onward, which is what a drift extrapolation looks like when the realised run outpaces the drift estimated over the preceding three years. The yellow fan behind them is the model's own statement of how wide the uncertainty is.

  ![[Pasted image 20260919234001.png]]

- **No error metric of any kind is reported for the paper's own LSTM.** This is worth being careful about, because the paper does quote RMSE figures of 0.00093 and 0.00859 at 500 epochs on High, Low, Open and Close, and those belong to the NIFTY 50 study it reviews in its literature section, not to the system it builds. Read quickly, the numbers look like results. They are not.
- **The requirements set is the durable contribution.** Live and summarised transaction dashboard, PDF summary report on the current portfolio, email and SMS notification on profits and losses incurred, multi-exchange portfolio composition, candlestick analysis filtered to monthly, weekly, 30, 90, 180 and 360 day slots, a wish stock list for holdings intended for future purchase, per-share forecast as a line graph, and account self-service, against an administrator side covering user analytics, active session overview and user provisioning. That list came out of 233 investor responses and interviews with the exchange, and it is reusable independently of anything else in the paper.

#### Limitations

- **The title claims the Colombo Stock Exchange and the evaluation never touches it.** Both prediction models are fed from Yahoo Finance and Tiingo, and every reported number comes from AAPL, MSFT or NKE. Whether either model behaves the same way on a thinly traded Colombo counter with long gaps between trades, wide spreads and price bands is the question the paper is titled to answer and does not test, and on this evidence I cannot carry any of its forecasting claims across to CSE.
- **Percentage difference between one realised price and one predicted price is not a forecasting metric.** There is no stated test window, no out-of-sample discipline, no naive baseline such as last price or drift-free random walk, and no per-horizon breakdown, so an 18.51% gap on Apple is reported inside a claim of more than 80% accuracy by treating the complement of a single error as an accuracy score. The two accuracy claims in the paper, more than 85% and more than 80%, are also not reconciled with each other.
- **Geometric Brownian motion assumes constant drift, constant volatility and log-normal returns, which is a poor fit for what the CSE literature actually reports.** [[Modeling Long-Term Volatility Memory Dynamics in the Colombo Stock Exchange]] finds persistent long memory in ASPI volatility across all three regimes it tests, and [[Sri Lankan Stock Market Volatility Analysis, An ARMA-GARCH Approach]] finds a significant leverage effect and persistence close to unity on ASPI from 2018 to 2022. Constant volatility estimated over a rolling three years is exactly the assumption both of those findings argue against, and the paper does not test the assumption or compare against a GARCH-family alternative.
- **The LSTM is not reproducible and not evaluated.** No architecture detail beyond the layer types, no lookback window, no train and test dates, no validation curve, no error figure. Since it is the model serving the 1 to 7 and 15 to 30 day horizons, which are the horizons an investor actually trades on, the untested component is the operationally important one.
- **Test scores are self-assessed and the acceptance testing has no independent user sample.** The scores in the test table have no stated rubric, the pass criteria are not defined, and UAT was run by the author together with CSE officials rather than by any of the 233 surveyed investors, which is a missed opportunity given the sample was already recruited.
- **Nothing in the system addresses the market it is built for.** No transaction costs, no brokerage fees, no liquidity or thin-trading treatment, no price band handling, and no rebalancing or allocation logic. The platform records and displays a portfolio and forecasts each holding separately, so despite the word adaptive in the title, there is no optimisation, no risk model and no allocation decision anywhere in it.

#### Why this matters for my project

- **This is my prior-art citation for the claim that CSE has no first-party portfolio and prediction platform, and it is also the reason I should not repeat that claim in the same form.** The honest framing in my proposal is that a CSE-targeted platform was proposed and built in 2021, that it was accepted by the exchange at the demonstration level, and that its forecasting component was never validated on Colombo-listed equities, which leaves the substantive question open rather than settled.
- **The officially provided XML endpoint changes how I approach the data access question.** My critical unknown is how much history `cse.lk` will actually return and whether derived data can be published, and this paper is evidence that a formal request through CSE R&D or the CIO has been granted to a local university project before. I should make that request in writing early, ask about retention depth and reuse terms in the same letter, and keep archiving daily in parallel regardless of the answer, since the past cannot be re-fetched.
- **The 233-respondent requirement set saves me a full elicitation round on the generic features.** Dashboard, PDF reporting, notification on realised profit and loss, watchlist, candlestick views over fixed windows and per-share forecast display are settled expectations for a CSE investor-facing tool, so my own elicitation should spend its budget on what is specific to my build, the explanations attached to a recommendation, the sentiment inputs and how much of the reasoning an investor wants surfaced.
- **The gap between more than 85% claimed and 18.51% observed on a single stock is the concrete argument for fixing the evaluation protocol before writing any model code.** Test window, metric, per-stock reporting and a naive baseline all get pinned down in my protocol document first, and results get reported per counter rather than as one aggregate, precisely because aggregation is what let a three-stock spread become an accuracy headline here.
- **What survives from the modelling is the horizon split and a baseline, not a method.** Serving different horizons with different models is sound, though the 1 to 7 and 15 to 30 day windows here are asserted and the 8 to 14 day gap is never explained, so if I split horizons the cut points should follow the volatility persistence evidence on CSE rather than round numbers. GBM itself belongs in my design as a baseline, cheap and transparent, which anything I build has to beat alongside buy-and-hold on the same CSE window before it earns its complexity.
- **A forecast shown to a CSE investor without costs and liquidity attached is a number they cannot act on.** In a thin, whale-dominated book, a predicted price is only usable alongside an estimate of what it costs to get in and out at that size, so any prediction surface I build carries execution assumptions with it rather than leaving the investor to discover them.
- **The forecast here is a line on a chart with no reasoning attached, which is the contrast my explainability angle needs.** This platform can tell an investor what it predicts and nothing about why, and since my stack layers language models, econometric volatility estimates and a learned allocation policy, the thing that distinguishes it from this one is not accuracy on a backtest, it is whether an investor can see which layer moved the recommendation and why.

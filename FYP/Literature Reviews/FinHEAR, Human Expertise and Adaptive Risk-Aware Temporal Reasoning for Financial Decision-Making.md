Findings of EMNLP 2025, pages 1648–1672, Chen, Zou, Wang, Wang, Sun, Zhang and Xu out of Fudan University, Tensorpacific, Peng Cheng Laboratory, Meta AI and the Shanghai Academy of AI for Science. A six-agent LLM framework for daily single-asset trading and multi-asset allocation, organised as an event-driven pipeline, historical trend into current event into expert retrieval, with a risk agent and a decision agent predicting magnitude and direction separately before they are combined into one of four actions, and a refinement agent that rewrites earlier analyses once outcomes land. Evaluated on three US equities and gold over calendar 2023 against nine baselines. Code released at github.com/pilgrim00/FinHEAR.

The reason to read this one alongside [[FinMe, A Performance-Enhanced LLM Trading Agent With Layered Memory and Character Design]] and [[FinCon, A Synthesized LLM Multi-Agent System with Conceptual Verbal Reinforcement for Enhanced Financial Decision Making]], both of which it uses as baselines, is that it is the only system in my set that makes <font color="#ffc000">position size a first-class output rather than a fixed one-share convention</font>. FinMem trades one share and reports a direction score wearing a trading metric's clothes, FinCon updates beliefs verbally but still sizes flat, and FinHEAR samples a risk sensitivity from a skewed Beta and multiplies it against expert reliability and event similarity to get a continuous weight. That single mechanism is the part I would actually reimplement, and the rest of the paper, the expert knowledge base and the behavioural-economics framing, is the argument for why the weight is shaped the way it is.

> [!NOTE]
> **What "human expertise" means here.** Not human-in-the-loop, and not annotation. The authors scrape a corpus of investor writing, Graham, Buffett, Soros, Lynch, Munger, Marks, Dalio, shareholder letters, interviews, biographies and documentaries, then use GPT-4o to compress it into structured *query–knowledge* pairs, an abstracted investment problem paired with a reasoning trajectory in that investor's idiom. Retrieval at decision time matches the current market state against those queries. So the expertise is frozen reasoning patterns pulled into a prompt, closer to [[Financial Report Chunking for Effective Retrieval Augmented Generation]]'s structure-aware retrieval than to anything human-supervised.

#### Gap it's addressing

- **Existing methods have no structured channel for accumulated human judgement.** Rule-based strategies encode expert heuristics but cannot generalise out of the regime they were written for, deep models learn patterns with no mechanism for the priors a practitioner brings, and the LLM agents that do reason in text reason from the prompt rather than from a curated body of prior decisions. The framing is information asymmetry, that markets contain unevenly distributed information and the agent should be able to reach for someone else's.
- **Risk sensitivity is flat where behavioural finance says it should be asymmetric.** Prospect theory holds that losses weigh more than equivalent gains, and the paper's charge is that existing agents ignore this, trading at constant exposure regardless of how uncertain the current read is. This is the gap the position-sizing mechanism answers, and it is the one that is genuinely under-addressed in the rest of my set.
- **Financial decisions unfold over time and early errors compound.** Static reasoning benchmarks score a single response, whereas a trading agent that misreads a macro event in March carries that reading forward unless something forces a revision. Hence the refinement agent, which rewrites historical trend summaries and expert entries once realised outcomes contradict them.

The first two are the load-bearing ones. The third is real but the paper never separates revision-for-accuracy from the simpler benefit of having any feedback signal at all, and the ablation cannot tell those apart either.

#### Data and setup

The dataset spans 26 December 2019 to 29 December 2023, split into training from 2 January 2020 to 29 December 2022 and testing across calendar 2023, with a 7-day lookback reserved on both ends so no prediction day ever sees future text. Four assets, AAPL, TSLA, XOM and gold (XAUUSD), chosen across sectors plus one commodity. The text side is the interesting part and it is large, 21,329 industry and company news items, 22,274 current-affairs articles, 2,128 macroeconomic data releases, 280 central-bank communications including full FOMC minutes and 61 IMF, G20 and national fiscal reports, against only 1,023 and 1,035 daily OHLCV rows for equities and gold respectively.

Each data type is routed to the agent that handles that temporal structure, which is the organising principle of the whole pipeline, and the ratio above is worth holding onto: <font color="#ffc000">roughly forty text items per trading day against one price row</font>. All LLM methods including FinHEAR run on GPT-4o-mini for fairness, while knowledge-base construction and the refinement step use full GPT-4o, and retrieval embeddings come from `text-embedding-3-large`. Every model gets a fixed 5-day input window in the main experiments. Baselines are momentum and mean reversion for rules, ALSTM, Informer, StockNet and PPO for deep learning, and CoT, a generative agent, FinMem and FinCon for LLM agents, with Markowitz mean-variance and DQN added for the portfolio task. Metrics are accuracy and MCC for forecasting, and cumulative return, Sharpe, max drawdown and Calmar for trading, all defined conventionally in Appendix A.2.

#### Model architecture and training

![[Pasted image 20260919230001.png]]

Six agents run in sequence. The **Historical Trend Agent** summarises price dynamics, volatility shifts and cyclical behaviour across horizons and has its summaries overwritten later by realised outcomes. The **Current Event Agent** interprets macro announcements and firm news against that context, judging both short-term impact and structural implication. The **Human Expertise Agent** encodes the current market state and retrieves the nearest expert case by cosine similarity over embeddings, a plain nearest-neighbour lookup that needs no equation to describe. The **Risk Analysis Agent** measures disagreement across those three and emits a discrete low, medium or high level with a rationale. The **Decision Agent** predicts direction independently. The **Temporal Refinement Agent** closes the loop, taking failed predictions and rewriting the expert entry or trend summary that produced them.

The risk level then drives sizing, and this is the mechanism I would need to reimplement rather than paraphrase. A sensitivity score is drawn from a Beta rescaled onto a context-specific interval, then multiplied against expert reliability and event similarity:

$$\rho \sim \text{ScaledBeta}(\alpha_r, \beta_r; [a_r, b_r]), \qquad w = f(\rho) \cdot h(\alpha) \cdot g(\gamma)$$

where:
- low-risk contexts draw from $\text{Beta}(5,2)$ on $[0.75, 0.9]$, left-skewed, encouraging moderate risk-taking in what the framework reads as a gain region
- high-risk contexts draw from $\text{Beta}(2,5)$ on $[0.1, 0.4]$, right-skewed, amplifying caution in the loss region
- $h(\alpha) = \max(\alpha, \epsilon_\alpha)$ floors expert reliability so a weak retrieval cannot zero the position outright
- $g(\gamma) = \sqrt{\max(\gamma, \epsilon_\gamma)}$ expands low event-similarity values so partially relevant precedents are not over-penalised

The asymmetry between the two Beta shapes is the entire prospect-theory implementation, and it is worth being clear that this is an assumed functional form rather than a calibration to any estimated loss-aversion parameter. The weight then meets the predicted direction $d$ through two thresholds:

$$a = \begin{cases} \text{close}, & w > \delta_{\text{high}} \\ \text{hold}, & w < \delta_{\text{low}} \\ \text{long}, & d = \text{up and } \delta_{\text{low}} \le w \le \delta_{\text{high}} \\ \text{short}, & d = \text{down and } \delta_{\text{low}} \le w \le \delta_{\text{high}} \end{cases}$$

The lower threshold suppresses weak signals into inaction and the upper one reads extreme confidence as a reason to flatten rather than to press, which is the loss-aversion asymmetry appearing a second time at the action layer. For the portfolio setting each asset's confidence score is turned into an allocation by a temperature-scaled softmax, $w_i = \exp(s_i/T) / \sum_j \exp(s_j/T)$, with lower $T$ sharpening concentration and higher $T$ forcing diversification.

There is no gradient training anywhere. The training period exists to populate and refine the expert knowledge base and the trend summaries, and refinement continues through the test period using realised outcomes as they arrive.

#### Findings

![[Pasted image 20260919230002.png]]

- **FinHEAR takes the highest accuracy and MCC on all four assets, and the absolute levels are the part worth quoting.** Accuracy of 58.97%, 59.08%, 61.56% and 58.19% on XAUUSD, AAPL, TSLA and XOM, against the best baseline on each of 56.81 (FinCon), 56.13 (CoT), 58.82 (FinMem) and 56.52 (PPO), with MCC of 0.2017, 0.1964, 0.1897 and 0.1625 against a baseline field mostly between 0.05 and 0.18. The margin is consistent, two to three accuracy points and a clear MCC gap, and the level is still under 62% on a binary next-day call. This is a real improvement over the field on this test year, not a solved problem, and the MCC figures are the honest summary of it.
- **The trading gains are return-driven, and FinHEAR never posts the lowest drawdown on any asset.** Cumulative returns of 32.45%, 30.81%, 58.74% and 37.16% with Sharpe of 1.84, 1.95, 1.62 and 2.25, best in both columns everywhere. Drawdown tells a different story, 23.88% on AAPL against the generative agent's 15.02%, 25.21% on TSLA against ALSTM's 23.71%, 29.61% on XOM against mean reversion's 12.90%. For a framework whose stated motivation is loss aversion, <font color="#ffc000">the risk mechanism is buying return per unit of risk rather than reducing risk</font>, which the Sharpe and Calmar columns support and the drawdown column quietly qualifies.
- **The portfolio result is the largest in the paper and it is the risk module carrying it.** Cumulative return of 83.89% with Sharpe 3.28 and Calmar 5.32, against FinCon's 55.02/2.11/1.57, DQN's 45.13/1.60/1.38 and Markowitz at 39.21/2.75/3.05. Markowitz holds the lowest drawdown at 12.84% against FinHEAR's 15.77%, so the classical baseline is not embarrassed on risk, it is beaten on return by a factor of two. Since the softmax allocation is the only thing added over the single-asset design, and removing adaptive risk-awareness costs 47.7 points of return here against 14.5 on AAPL, the sizing mechanism matters far more at portfolio level than within one asset.
- **Every component removal hurts, and a separate two-path ablation shows the curated knowledge is worth as much as the architecture.**

  ![[Pasted image 20260919230003.png]]

  On AAPL the largest single-asset drop is removing past trend, 30.81% to 14.41%, followed by adaptive risk-awareness at 16.32% and human expertise at 19.75%; on the portfolio the order inverts, with adaptive risk-awareness the costliest at 36.18% against 83.89%. Separately, giving plain chain-of-thought the full knowledge base lifts it from 13.98 to 18.19 on XAUUSD and from −14.26 to 10.37 on TSLA, while FinHEAR's architecture with a keyword-only knowledge base still reaches 24.33, 19.55 and 39.26. Both halves contribute and neither is sufficient, which is a cleaner disentangling than FinMem ever ran on its own layering.
- **It stays profitable through the 2023 rate-hike downtrend, and the accompanying sentence overstates the table it cites.** From 5 May to 5 October 2023 FinHEAR returns 7.82% with Sharpe 0.47 while every baseline is negative, FinCon at −5.58, ALSTM at −10.39, CoT at −10.12 and FinMem at −8.98, which is the most convincing robustness evidence in the paper. In the preceding risk-aversion uptrend it holds the best Sharpe at 4.79, but CoT returns 19.38% against FinHEAR's 18.25%, so the claim of highest returns *and* Sharpe in both regimes is not what Table 3 shows. The downtrend result survives the correction intact and is the one I would cite.
- **Expert style and input window both align with asset character, which the paper names Temporal Specificity of Asset Performance.**

  ![[Pasted image 20260919230004.png]]

  Soros-style on gold, Buffett on AAPL, Lynch on TSLA and Graham on XOM each top their asset, and the window study puts gold and TSLA's best at 3 days, AAPL at 5 and XOM at 7, the authors reading this as event-driven assets peaking on short horizons and macro-cyclical ones on longer. The mechanism is plausible and the evidence is one asset per cell on one test year, so I would treat it as a hypothesis worth designing an experiment around rather than a result.

#### Limitations

- **One test year, one split, no repeated runs and no significance testing anywhere.** Calendar 2023 is a single regime sequence, and every number in every table is a single trajectory, with no seeds, no dispersion and no walk-forward. FinMem at least averaged five trials and ran Wilcoxon signed-rank against the second-best model, and FinHEAR reports neither, so an eight-point cumulative-return gap on TSLA has no attached statement about how much of it would survive resampling. Given that the whole system is a stochastic LLM pipeline with a Beta draw inside the sizing rule, run-to-run variance is not a hypothetical concern, it is built into the architecture.
- **The results tables disagree with each other, and every disagreement favours the headline.** Table 7's best expert-style row should be the same run as Table 2's FinHEAR row, and on three of four assets it is not: TSLA reads 48.74/35.21 against 58.74/25.21, XOM reads 27.16 against 37.16, and gold's drawdown reads 28.47 against 18.47. Each table is internally consistent, since Calmar recomputes correctly from its own cells in both, and gold's Calmar of 1.14 in Table 2 is only reachable from a drawdown of 28.47, which means the headline drawdown figure is the one that cannot be right. Table 11 adds a fourth, 58.47 for TSLA where everything else says 58.74. I am reporting the arithmetic rather than a cause, and the direction is consistent enough that the main table should not be quoted without the appendix beside it.
- **The hyperparameters that operationalise prospect theory are selected on the test window.** The threshold grid over $\delta_{\text{low}}$ and $\delta_{\text{high}}$ is scored on 2023 cumulative return and Sharpe, the optimum at (0.2, 0.6) reproduces the reported AAPL figures exactly, and the paper then reads that optimum as aligning with prospect theory's prediction of stronger aversion in loss domains. That is a fit to the evaluation period being presented as theoretical confirmation. The same applies to the 3, 5 and 7-day window comparison and to expert-style assignment, none of which uses a validation split, and it is the same class of problem as FinMem's best-of-three risk inclination, differing only in that FinMem said so in the text.
- **No transaction costs in the main results, and the cost check is one asset at a rate no thin market would offer.** The headline numbers are gross, and the robustness appendix applies 5 basis points per side on AAPL only, taking cumulative return from 30.81% to 25.96% and Sharpe from 1.95 to 1.68. That is a 16% haircut on return at a cost level bracketed below MacroHFT's 2 bps and far below the 33 to 40 bps fee fixed point [[FreQuant, A Reinforcement-Learning based Adaptive Portfolio Optimization with Multi-frequency Decomposition]] solves for, and turnover is never reported anywhere, so the haircut cannot be rescaled to another cost regime from what is printed.
- **All LLM baselines were re-implemented on GPT-4o-mini, and at least two of them post results their own papers do not.** FinCon returns −8.17% on AAPL and FinMem −20.15% on XOM, both large negatives for systems published with positive results on US large caps. The fairness argument for holding the backbone constant is sound, but FinMem's own backbone ablation swung from +62.62% to −52.72% across models, so a shared weak backbone is not a neutral control, it is a condition that may affect the baselines and FinHEAR unequally, and nothing in this paper tests that. The comparison is best read as *under GPT-4o-mini*, not as a ranking of the designs.
- **The behavioural grounding is asserted at the level of functional form, and the paper concedes as much.** Beta shapes of (5,2) and (2,5), the intervals [0.75, 0.9] and [0.1, 0.4], the floors $\epsilon_\alpha$ and $\epsilon_\gamma$ and the square root in $g(\gamma)$ are all hand-set with no search reported and no reference to any estimated prospect-theory parameter. The Limitations section says the integration between AI reasoning and human cognitive theories remains preliminary, which is the right characterisation, and it means the theory is supplying vocabulary and a sign convention here rather than constraining the model.

#### Why this matters for my project

- **Continuous position sizing driven by a risk state is the mechanism I am taking, and it is the piece missing from every other agent note in the vault.** FinMem trades one share, FinCon sizes flat and reasons about risk verbally, [[AlphaAgents, Large Language Model Based Multi-Agents for Equity Portfolio Constructions]] allocates equally across selected names. FinHEAR is the only one where the uncertainty estimate becomes a number the trade is multiplied by, and the multiplicative form, confidence times source reliability times precedent similarity, is cheap enough to implement in an afternoon and decomposable enough that each factor can be swapped. That decomposability is what makes it a template rather than a result.
- **Replacing the sampled $\rho$ with a volatility-state estimate is the obvious substitution and it connects two parts of my design.** The Beta draw is a stand-in for a risk state the system cannot otherwise measure, and on the CSE I can measure one: the persistence estimates in [[Modeling Long-Term Volatility Memory Dynamics in the Colombo Stock Exchange]] give a conditioning variable with actual econometric content, and [[MacroHFT, Memory Augmented Context-aware Reinforcement Learning On High Frequency Trading]] already demonstrates volatility used as a conditioning variable a policy trains against rather than a metric reported at the end. The experiment that follows is a direct one, FinHEAR's sampled sensitivity against a persistence-conditioned one on the same pipeline, and it is the cleanest way I have to show the econometric layer is earning its place instead of arguing it from theory.
- **The expert knowledge base does not transfer, and the honest version of it for the CSE is a different corpus with the same shape.** There is no Sri Lankan equivalent of Berkshire letters and no local investor canon in English or Sinhala, so lifting Buffett and Graham wholesale imports reasoning patterns calibrated to deep, liquid, heavily disclosed markets into a thin one. What does transfer is the query–knowledge abstraction: broker research notes, annual report chairman statements and CSE-specific commentary can be compressed into the same paired structure. The sizing of that corpus is the open question, since this paper never reports how many pairs it built or how retrieval degrades when the nearest precedent is far away.
- **The event-centric routing suits the CSE better than the price-centric designs do, and the data ratio says why.** Forty text items per price row is closer to the CSE's information profile than to anything in the DRL literature, where the assumption is long, dense price histories. Given that historical data depth is the unresolved question gating the whole project, a design whose dominant input is dated text rather than a decade of bars is the one that survives the bad case, and the macro channel maps cleanly onto CBSL monetary policy reviews and budget announcements standing in for the FOMC minutes here.
- **Cost realism is where this design has to be stress-tested before I build on it, and the test is cheap.** Five basis points per side costs 16% of AAPL's return; the CSE charges materially more and the book is thin enough that market impact is a separate cost on top. Since FinHEAR emits four actions including close, and closing and reopening is a round trip, the strategy's turnover is high by construction and unreported, so the first thing I would instrument is a turnover counter and a cost sweep across CSE-realistic levels. <font color="#ff0000">If the sizing mechanism only pays at 5 bps, it is not the mechanism I want</font>, and I would rather find that out on their architecture than on mine.
- **The threshold rule gives me an abstention path, which is a requirement rather than a refinement in my setting.** When the combined weight falls below $\delta_{\text{low}}$ the agent holds, so there is an explicit structural route to not trading when the evidence is weak, which is exactly the gap I flagged in the FinMem note where top-$K$ retrieval returns stale news regardless of score. On the CSE, where many tickers will have one weak signal or none on a given day and the sentiment–return relationship is conditional on liquidity per [[Role of Market Liquidity in Sentiment-Based Return Predictions, Evidence from Sri Lanka]], an inaction branch triggered by low retrieval quality is the first thing I would wire in, and this paper shows it costs one comparison.
- **Both the style-asset alignment and the window study are real design questions that this paper answers by fitting to the test set, and I should answer them the same way but on validation.** Per-asset input windows and per-asset reasoning styles are plausible enough that I want them in the design, and the selection has to happen on a held-out period with the test year untouched, otherwise the reported gain is the selection. This is the same discipline problem as the absent CSE benchmark, and it argues for fixing the split protocol before any of these choices get made rather than after.
- **Holding the backbone constant across baselines is right, and it has to be reported as a condition rather than a control.** For my own comparisons the practical rule this yields is to run the final table on at least two backbones of different strength, since a single weak one can compress the field and a single strong one can hide a design's dependence on it, and my multilingual requirement means the model handling Sinhala and code-mixed input will not be the strongest reasoner available. FinMem established that backbone choice can swing a result by a hundred points; FinHEAR shows what happens to interpretation when only one is tried.

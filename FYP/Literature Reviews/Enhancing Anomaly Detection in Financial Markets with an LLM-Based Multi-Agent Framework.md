arXiv:2403.19735v1 [q-fin.RM], Taejin Park, 28 March 2024. A single-author preprint, affiliated to the Bank for International Settlements but carrying the standard disclaimer that the views are the author's alone, so it is not a BIS Working Paper and should not be cited as one. It proposes an LLM multi-agent framework that sits *after* a conventional anomaly detector and automates the manual verification that normally follows an alert. A statistical screen flags outliers in a tabular series, a question-formulating agent converts the flagged rows plus their metadata into natural-language verification questions, three specialised expert agents answer them from different evidence sources, a consolidation agent writes a summary report, a panel of three manager personas plus a moderator and a critic discusses it through AutoGen, and a human analyst receives the result. Demonstrated once, on daily S&P 500 percentage changes from 1980 to 2023, against three genuine outliers and three deliberately injected missing values. There are no metrics anywhere in the paper.

This note replaces the one-line tabling I left in August, and that instinct was about half right. The paper is not about investing and it will not be a source for any agent in my architecture, but the stub's other half, that the tooling might be useful, understates it. Two things here bear directly on the orchestrator: the expert agents are required to answer in a fixed three-label vocabulary, which is the closest thing in my set to comparable outputs across heterogeneous producers, and the one case where the agents disagreed gets resolved by evidence type rather than by vote or by discussion. Both are read here against [[Scope sentiment analysis dropped]].

> [!NOTE] What is being detected, and by what
> Nothing in the multi-agent framework detects anomalies. Detection is done beforehand by an ordinary z-score screen, and the agents only adjudicate whether a flagged point is a real market event, a data error, or unresolvable. The title reads as though the LLMs are doing the detecting, and they are not, they are doing alert triage. The distinction matters because it is the triage step, not the detection step, that has any bearing on my work.

#### Major differences to mine

The obvious one is the task, this validates data and does not act on it, so there are no positions, no returns, no transaction costs and no evaluation protocol of any kind. The deeper one is the axis of heterogeneity. My four agents are heterogeneous by analytical lens, $A_T$ technical, $A_F$ fundamental and retrieval, $A_V$ volatility and risk, $A_L$ liquidity, each answering a different question about the same instrument, and their outputs have to be combined into one decision. Park's three experts are heterogeneous by *evidence source*, web search, a curated knowledge base and an external vendor feed, and all three answer the *same* question. That is redundancy for verification rather than diversity for decision, and it means agreement between his agents is meant to be corroboration, where agreement between mine would be a weak signal at best. Meant to be, because the sources turn out not to be disjoint, which the limitations take up. The third difference is the terminal step, a briefing to a human analyst who makes the call, where my orchestrator has to emit the decision itself, so nothing here tells me how to close the loop.

And as in [[TradingAgents, Multi-Agents LLM Financial Trading Framework]] and [[FinVision, A Multi-Agent Framework for Stock Market Prediction]], there is no weight, no confidence scalar and no quantity anywhere that could be weighted. Consolidation is an LLM summarising free text.

#### Standout features

All the paper says about the screen is that it applies the z-score method to the daily percentage changes of the S&P 500 series with a deliberately high threshold of 10 z-scores, and that this identifies three outliers. It gives no equation and does not say over what window the moments are taken, so the form below is my reconstruction rather than anything the paper states,

$$z_t = \frac{r_t - \mu_r}{\sigma_r}, \qquad \text{flag if } |z_t| > 10$$

where $r_t$ is the daily percentage change and $\mu_r$, $\sigma_r$ are the mean and standard deviation over the whole 1980 to 2023 sample. That full-sample reading is an inference, and the arithmetic supports it, since $\sigma_r$ over that span is roughly 1.1 % and so the threshold sits near an 11 % move, which is exactly where the cut falls: $-20.47$ on 19 October 1987, $+11.58$ on 13 October 2008 and $-11.98$ on 16 March 2020 are flagged, while the roughly 9 % days visible either side of them in Figure 2 are not. A rolling or conditional window would not produce that pattern. Three nulls are then injected at 20 October 1987, 14 October 2008 and 12 March 2020, and the third of those *precedes* its outlier rather than following it, which matters later.

Four design details are worth keeping. <font color="#ffc000">Every expert agent is instructed to begin its answer with Correct, Incorrect or Uncertain and then give the reasoning in one sentence</font>, which is a fixed, comparable output vocabulary imposed across agents that share nothing else. Metadata travels with the data, argued in prose as the data's name, source, frequency, description and coverage, and instantiated in Table 1 as six JSON keys, `original_frequency_code`, `CURRENCY`, `DATA_DESCR`, `PRICING_SOURCE`, `REF_AREA` and `TITLE`, and the paper's strongest argument is that raw tabular values are illegible to an LLM without them. The cross-check runs against a genuinely separate vendor, since the series itself is Bloomberg, `BB:D:SPX_INDEX:PX_LAST`, and the verification pulls Yahoo Finance, with the bridge between them a generated identifier from the literal prompt `f"What is the yahoo finance ticker for {meta_data['TITLE']}? Please just return the ticker."`. And the framework is explicitly open at the expert layer, the diagram has a fourth empty box, so the expert set is meant to be configured per dataset rather than fixed. The web research agent runs on a search platform given as Tavily, named illustratively in the text as "such as Tavily" but with a footnote pointing at the product, so it is the engine actually used.

#### Findings

- **All three experts independently returned Correct on the three genuine outliers, each citing a different kind of evidence, and the test is easy by construction.** Web research grounded 1987 in Federal Reserve history and the Dow's 22.6 % fall, 2008 in a CNN report of an 11.8 % gain described as the index's second-best daily move, and 2020 in CNBC's 12 % single-day drop. Institutional knowledge reached the same three verdicts from a curated crisis document, and cross-checking matched the values against Yahoo Finance. Three paths converging is the demonstration's one clean result and it is worth very little, partly because only two of the three are actually independent, and mostly because Black Monday, the October 2008 rebound and the COVID crash are three of the most documented days in market history and all three sit inside the backbone's pre-training. <font color="#ffc000">The framework is never shown an anomaly it could plausibly get wrong.</font>
- **The injected nulls were the only discriminating case, and exactly one agent handled it.** Web research returned Uncertain, saying the searches gave no information about the absence of data. Institutional knowledge returned Uncertain, saying that without information on trading suspensions or reporting errors it could not explain the gap. The cross-checking agent went to the API and found the values were 236.83, 998.01 and 2711.02, so the nulls were the injection and not a property of the series. On this evidence the framework's entire diagnostic value came from the one agent with access to an independent structured source, and neither the LLM reasoning nor the discussion layer contributed to it.

  ![[Pasted image 20260926010003.png]]

- **The consolidation agent resolved the disagreement by deferring to the harder evidence rather than averaging three views.** Item 4 of the summary report reads that while initially uncertain whether the nulls indicated no recorded change or missing data, the cross-checking expert clarifies that the values were available, which negates the assumption of missing data. Two Uncertains and one grounded Correct produced a Correct, not a hedge. This is the most useful thing in the paper for me and it carries almost no weight on its own, being a single instance of behaviour emergent from a summarisation prompt rather than a specified rule, with nothing testing what happens when two agents assert contradictory Corrects.
- **The management discussion added no information, propagated an error, and the critic endorsed rather than challenged.** Across the transcript the financial market economist, macro economist and statistician each restate the same four findings in their own register, the moderator declares consensus, and the critic opens by commending the discussants and concludes there are no apparent doubts or concerns, after which five further turns of mutual thanks run on with the critic rather than the moderator having the last word. The error is the useful part. The macro economist describes the three null dates as "the dates following each event", and the moderator and the critic both repeat that phrasing, but 12 March 2020 *precedes* the 16 March outlier it is paired with, so the characterisation is wrong on a third of the cases. <font color="#ff0000">There was something in the report for a critic to catch, the critic's own turn restates it instead</font>, and the layer that consumes the most pages in the paper is the one that changes nothing about the output.

#### Limitations

- **There is no evaluation.** No precision or recall over a labelled set of alerts, no comparison against a human analyst, no baseline, no ablation, one series, one run. The abstract claims improved efficiency, improved accuracy and reduced human intervention, and the paper contains no measurement of any of the three. Everything here is a worked example, and I should cite it as an architecture proposal rather than as evidence for anything.
- **The hard case for alert triage is the one the paper concedes and does not attempt.** It notes that major news media or press releases may not cover every identified anomaly, which is why the null question came back Uncertain from both text-based experts. That is the real operating regime for anomaly validation, a mid-sized move in a lightly covered name with no press coverage, and it is exactly what the CSE looks like. On this evidence I cannot tell how the framework behaves there, only that two of its three experts would abstain.
- **On the full-sample reading, an unconditional z-score over 44 years is the wrong screen, and the choice of 10 hides the consequence.** Pooling $\sigma_r$ across quiet and turbulent regimes ignores volatility clustering, so the screen systematically under-flags in calm periods and over-flags in crises, and the threshold of 10 papers over this by returning only the three largest days in the sample. That makes the demonstration tractable and leaves the operational question untouched, because a threshold that produced hundreds of alerts a day is where cost, latency and triage quality would actually be tested, and there are no cost or runtime figures anywhere in the paper.
- **The multi-agent structure is never shown to be necessary, and the experts are not as separate as the design implies.** The institutional knowledge agent's instruction ends "Use web search to verify facts as needed", so experts 1 and 2 share a source and differ only in having a `.docx` knowledge base bolted on, and the paper concedes the consequence directly, noting that because the series is well known this agent produced similar responses to the web research agent. The third is thinner than it reads too: section 3.5 says the agent is programmed to access Yahoo Finance via API, while the Table 5 prompt asks it to cross-check "with data I provide in JSON", so the fetch is done by the surrounding Python and the agent only compares two blocks handed to it. The obvious baseline, one agent given search, the knowledge base and the fetched vendor data together, is not run. Given that the experts agreed on everything except the one question where only one of them had the comparison data, <font color="#ffc000">the case for separating them rests entirely on which tool output reached which prompt</font>, which is a plumbing argument rather than an agent-architecture argument.
- **The discussion layer's objective makes disagreement a failure state.** The stated aim of the AutoGen phase is reaching an agreement on the contents of the summary report, so a panel that converges immediately has satisfied its objective, and the moderator and critic roles have no incentive structure that would reward dissent. This is the same collapse as in TradingAgents' debate rounds but reached at much smaller scale and without a role hierarchy, so it does not look like an artefact of any one design.

#### Why this matters for my project

- **The Correct, Incorrect, Uncertain vocabulary is a candidate output contract for my agents, and the abstain label is the valuable part.** Most of my multi-agent set has agents emitting free text or a bare direction with nothing comparable across them. Forcing a small fixed label set plus one sentence of reasoning is cheap, and the third label carries information a forced signal destroys. In a thin market this is not hypothetical, an $A_F$ query against a company with no recent filing and an $A_L$ read on a stock that did not trade should both be able to say they have no basis rather than emitting a neutral number the orchestrator cannot distinguish from genuine neutrality. The proposed refinement to $C_{i,t}$ is that <font color="#ffc000">no evidence and low confidence are different states and should weight differently</font>, which is a change to the conditioning set rather than a tuning detail, and it belongs in the discussion with the supervisor about what $C_{i,t}$ actually is.
- **Resolution by evidence type, which is a possible structure on $f(\cdot)$ rather than a flat scalar.** The nulls case is the only worked example I have found of heterogeneous agents disagreeing and the disagreement being settled correctly, and it was settled because one channel had harder evidence in front of it, not because three agents talked. For my orchestrator this argues that the weighting function should know something about the *kind* of evidence behind each agent's output, since direct market data underneath $A_T$, $A_V$ and $A_L$ and retrieved report text underneath $A_F$ are not the same epistemic quality and collapsing them into one comparable confidence number assumes they are. I would put this to the supervisor as a question about whether $w_{i,t}$ should be partly structural rather than entirely learned, and flag that a single anecdote in a paper with no evaluation is thin evidence for it.
- **Metadata as the bridge for $A_F$, and a second independent argument for structure-aware chunking.** Park's clearest claim is that tabular values without name, source, frequency, currency and coverage are uninterpretable to an LLM, and that metadata is what lets pre-trained knowledge attach to the numbers. The same problem is worse for CSE filings, where a retrieved fragment has to arrive carrying company, period, statement type and currency or the model cannot tell a quarterly figure from an annual one or an adjusted figure from a reported one. This lines up with what [[Financial Report Chunking for Effective Retrieval Augmented Generation]] argues from retrieval accuracy and reaches it from a different direction, which makes it a firmer commitment for $A_F$ than either paper alone supports, and it is consistent with the metadata-carrying node structure in [[Neo4j Graph-RAG for CSE]].
- **The identifier-resolution step is a pattern to reuse and a trap to avoid.** Generating an external ticker from a series title with a model call and then grounding the answer through an API is the right shape, and it will not survive contact with the CSE, where company names are not in the model's pre-training the way S&P 500 INDEX is, names change after restructurings, and voting and non-voting classes of the same issuer make the mapping ambiguous rather than merely unknown. The decision is a maintained local name-to-ticker lookup built as part of the dataset work, and it belongs in the data preparation plan now rather than being discovered during implementation.
- **Another instance of a discussion layer failing as a coordination mechanism, at the smallest scale I have seen it.** TradingAgents reaches the same place with a seven-role hierarchy and many more tokens, so the collapse does not look like an artefact of either design, and that strengthens the case for a fitted weight vector over any conversational aggregation. If deliberation ever enters my design the commitment is that it is scored on something other than whether the agents converged, with the pairwise conflict scoring in [[Explainable zero-shot trading using multi-agent LLM architecture, A backtested approach for Bitcoin price]] the only workable alternative I have found.
- **The unconditional screen is a concrete case where $A_V$ and $A_L$ have to be read jointly.** Standardising a return by a pooled 44-year $\sigma$ judges a move against the wrong regime, and my project already commits to conditional volatility through FIGARCH and GJR-GARCH, so the natural correction is standardising by conditional volatility, which is what [[Modeling Long-Term Volatility Memory Dynamics in the Colombo Stock Exchange]] supports for this market. In the CSE the correction is not sufficient on its own, because a stock that has not traded for several days produces a large printed return on its next trade that is a staleness artefact rather than a volatility event, and telling those apart is $A_L$'s job. So an $A_V$ flag on a thin name is uninterpretable without the $A_L$ state alongside it, which is a case against treating the four channels as fully independent producers and an argument that at least this pair needs a defined interaction.
- **The most likely place this paper ends up in my work is data quality, not architecture.** The outstanding blocker is a long, clean, legally usable individual-stock CSE series, and thin markets produce exactly the pathologies this framework triages, missing days, stale prints, unadjusted corporate actions and duplicated records, so a scaled-down version of the loop run over the CSE data before any experiment is a reasonable use of it. Two channels rather than five, a conditional-volatility screen for candidates and a cross-check against the exchange's own published data, with the Correct, Incorrect, Uncertain contract retained so the output is auditable. That is a data preparation utility and a line in the methodology chapter, not a contribution, and it should be written up that way.

arXiv:2412.20138v7, Yijia Xiao, Edward Sun and Wei Wang of UCLA with Di Luo of MIT, under the Tauric Research banner, last revised 3 June 2025. Seven LLM agent roles arranged as a simulated trading firm, four analysts covering fundamentals, sentiment, news and technicals, a bullish and a bearish researcher who debate for $n$ rounds, a trader, a three-perspective risk team of aggressive, neutral and conservative agents, and a fund manager who authorises the trade. Everything runs on the ReAct prompting framework over a shared agent state, communication between teams is by structured report rather than free-form message passing, and the backbone is tiered, `gpt-4o-mini` and `gpt-4o` for retrieval and summarisation and `o1-preview` for decisions. Backtested from 1 January to 29 March 2024 on AAPL, GOOGL and AMZN against buy-and-hold and four rule-based strategies, on cumulative return, annualised return, Sharpe and maximum drawdown. Code is public.

This is the paper the original project sketch was drawn from, and the note it replaces opened with the words "very similar to mine", which was accurate at the time and is the reason it needs rewriting rather than extending. What has changed is not the paper but my design. The role decomposition here is genuinely the ancestor of my four agents, but after the scope cut of 20 September ([[Scope sentiment analysis dropped]]) the thing I am building is defined by what happens *between* the agents, and this framework has nothing there at all, <font color="#ffc000">seven specialised roles feeding a fixed hierarchy with no weighting, no state conditioning and no ablation to show that any of the structure contributes anything</font>. So it moves from methodological template to contrast case, and it is the strongest one in my set, because unlike the fixed-influence failures I have collected elsewhere this system could not detect the problem even if it were happening.

> [!NOTE] ReAct as used here
> ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) interleaves reasoning traces with tool calls in a single prompt loop, so the model alternates between writing a thought and issuing an action, then reads the observation back before continuing. Every agent in TradingAgents runs this loop against the shared environment state, which is what makes the appendix transcripts readable as a decision log, each step shows the thought, the tool call, the arguments and the returned data. It is a prompting pattern, not a training method, so nothing in this framework is fitted to market data at any point.

#### Gap it's addressing

- **Existing agent frameworks optimise task performance and ignore how trading firms actually organise a decision.** The charge is that prior systems attach agents to narrow subtasks disconnected from the operating procedures that work in practice, so they never benefit from the division of labour that a real desk uses. This is the stated motivation for the whole design and it is the one with no evidence behind it, since nothing in the experiments separates the firm structure from a flat pool of the same agents.
- **Natural language as the sole communication medium corrupts state over long horizons.** The "telephone effect", where detail is lost and earlier context is buried as message histories lengthen, and where an unstructured pool of information forces every exchange to depend on retrieval. This is the concrete and correct part of the diagnosis, and the structured-report protocol is a real answer to it rather than a restatement of the problem.
- **Deep learning trading systems are opaque at the point where opacity is most expensive.** Dense architectures give no account of why a position was taken, which matters when an incorrect decision moves real capital. The response is that an LLM system produces its reasoning as a by-product, and the paper backs this up by printing a full day's trading log across sixteen appendix pages.

The second and third gaps are addressed by the design. The first is asserted throughout and tested nowhere, and since it is the framing the title rests on, the mismatch between the claimed contribution and the evidence starts here.

#### Data and setup

The backtest runs 1 January to 29 March 2024, roughly <font color="#ffc000">sixty trading days</font>, over a stated universe of Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta and Google, of which three are reported, AAPL, GOOGL and AMZN, so the reported set does not match the named set and Amazon appears in results without appearing in the universe. Decisions are made on data available up to each trading day, signals are executed, and metrics are computed before the loop advances, which is the paper's look-ahead control.

The multimodal dataset is wide. Historical OHLCV and adjusted close, daily news from Bloomberg, Yahoo, EODHD, FinnHub and Reddit, social posts from Reddit and X with sentiment scores computed by auxiliary language models, insider sentiment and transactions from SEDI and company filings, quarterly and annual reports, third-party company profiles and financial history, and <font color="#ffff00">sixty standard technical indicators per asset</font> including MACD, RSI and Bollinger Bands. Nothing is said about how the sixty indicators are selected down for a given day beyond the technical analyst choosing relevant ones, and the appendix transcript shows it picking eight.

Baselines are buy-and-hold, MACD, KDJ combined with RSI, zero mean reversion and SMA crossover, all rule-based and none of them an LLM, which is the single most consequential choice in the experimental design. Evaluation is on cumulative return, annualised return, Sharpe and maximum drawdown.

Two things are absent that a reader should not have to notice on their own. <font color="#ff0000">There is no transaction cost model anywhere, no fee, no slippage and no market impact</font>, and there is no repetition, so every reported number is a single run with no dispersion and no significance test. The backtest length is set by inference cost rather than by statistical adequacy, and the paper says so in a footnote, eleven LLM calls and more than twenty tool calls per prediction made three months the affordable window. Deployment needs no GPU, only API credits.

#### Model architecture and training

There is no training. Every agent is a prompted role with a fixed goal, a tool set and a set of constraints, and the only thing that changes between days is the data in the shared state.

The roles and what each one is responsible for producing, which is worth having laid out because the mapping onto my own agent set is the substance of the last section:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Fundamentals Analyst | Financial statements, earnings, insider transactions, an intrinsic-value read |
| Sentiment Analyst | Social media volume and polarity, insider sentiment, short-horizon crowd behaviour |
| News Analyst | News wires, government announcements, macro state and major world events |
| Technical Analyst | Selects from sixty indicators, computes them, reports momentum, trend, volatility and volume signals |
| Bullish Researcher | Argues the upside from the analyst reports, $n$ rounds of debate |
| Bearish Researcher | Argues the downside and the risks, same debate |
| Trader | Reads the debate outcome, issues a decision with a written rationale |
| Risk Team | Aggressive, neutral and conservative agents deliberate over the trader's plan and adjust it within risk constraints |
| Fund Manager | Reviews the risk discussion, applies adjustments, authorises execution |

The communication protocol is the real contribution. Analysts write structured reports into a global agent state and downstream roles query only the fields they need, which keeps the prompt from accumulating the whole conversation and is what the paper means by avoiding message corruption. Free-form natural language is confined to two bounded places, the bull versus bear debate and the risk team discussion, each run for $n$ rounds under a facilitator agent that reviews the history, <font color="#ffc000">selects the prevailing perspective</font> and records it back as a structured entry. The value of $n$ is never given.

That facilitator is the only point in the system where evidence from opposing agents is combined, and it is described in one sentence. There is no aggregation equation in the paper at all, which is consistent for a framework whose claim is organisational rather than algorithmic, and is adjudicated in limitations below.

Two of the four reported metrics carry the argument, so they are worth having explicitly. The Sharpe ratio is the standard excess-return-over-volatility form,

$$\text{SR} = \frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{\sigma}$$

where $\bar{R}$ is the average portfolio return, $R_f$ the risk-free rate and $\sigma$ the standard deviation of portfolio returns. The point of writing it out is that nothing in it is scaled to sample length, so a sixty-day window with few pullbacks drives $\sigma$ down and the ratio up without the strategy being better, which is exactly what happens below. Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline over the run,

$$\text{MDD} = \max_{t \in [0,T]} \left( \frac{\text{Peak}_t - \text{Trough}_t}{\text{Peak}_t} \right) \times 100\%$$

and it inherits the same problem from the other direction, a sixty-day window simply contains fewer opportunities to draw down, which is why every strategy in the table including the rule-based ones reports drawdowns under 13 %.

#### Findings

- **The reported margins are large on all three stocks and shrink sharply where the passive benchmark is strong.** Cumulative return of 26.62 % on AAPL, 24.36 % on GOOGL and 23.21 % on AMZN over the sixty-day window, against a best rule-based baseline of 2.05, 6.23 and 11.01 respectively. The improvement row is computed against the best baseline and gives 24.57, 16.58 and 6.10 points, and <font color="#ffc000">the 6.10 on AMZN is the honest number</font>, because AMZN is the only case where a baseline was doing well, buy-and-hold returning 17.1 %. Drawdown runs the other way, 0.91 / 1.69 / 2.11 against rule-based bests of 0.86 / 1.22 / 0.82, so the simple rules control risk better on all three and the paper concedes the trade-off directly.

  ![[Pasted image 20260925234002.png]]

- **The AAPL case is the one that would survive a harder test, because the benchmark there is negative.** Buy-and-hold returned $-5.23$ % on AAPL over the window and the framework returned 26.62 %, which cannot be explained by exposure to a rising asset, and the transaction history figure shows both long and short arrows so the gain includes short positions. This is the result the authors lead with and it is the right one to lead with.

  ![[Pasted image 20260925234003.png]]

  The AMZN trajectory is the useful counterpoint and is not discussed. The framework tracks buy-and-hold almost exactly for the first six weeks and separates only in mid-February, which on a sixty-day run means the entire reported edge on that stock comes from a few weeks.

- **The Sharpe ratios are not usable as reported, and the authors say so themselves.** Values of 8.21, 6.39 and 5.60 against an expected empirical range the paper states as above 2 being very good and above 3 excellent. The footnote attributes this to few pullbacks during the period, says the decision sequences were exported and checked for calculation correctness, and commits to longer backtests when budget allows. Reporting a number you believe is an artefact, and saying why, is better practice than quietly dropping it, but the consequence is that <font color="#ff0000">the Sharpe row cannot support the risk-adjusted-return claim the discussion section builds on it</font>, and the discussion makes that claim anyway.
- **The explainability result is the one that is actually demonstrated rather than argued, and the transcripts hold up to reading.** Sixteen appendix pages reproduce a complete day's log for AAPL on 19 November 2024, the market analyst's indicator selection, the tool calls with arguments and call IDs, the news and fundamentals reports, both research cases in full, all three risk perspectives and the fund manager's four-point rationale. The debate is not two agents talking past each other, the bear picks up the bull's liquidity claim and reads the same current and quick ratios below 1 as a risk rather than a buffer, and takes the same insider sales the bull dismissed as a signal. Whether any of that improves the decision is untested, but as an artefact this is the most transferable thing in the paper, and it costs nothing to produce because the reasoning is a by-product of the ReAct loop rather than a separate attribution step.

#### Limitations

- **Sixty trading days, three stocks, one regime, one run.** No repeats, no dispersion, no paired test, no held-out period, and a window chosen by inference budget rather than by what would make the comparison informative. Q1 2024 was a strong quarter for two of the three tickers, and the third supplies the paper's best result, which means the evidence base for a seven-agent framework is one favourable quarter and one adverse single-stock case. Nothing here is a claim about whether the framework works, it is that <font color="#ffc000">on this evidence the question has not been asked in a form that could return a negative answer</font>. [[Explainable zero-shot trading using multi-agent LLM architecture, A backtested approach for Bitcoin price]] runs 268 days across 20 seeds with standard deviations reported, and the consequence there is that the headline margin stops clearing its own error bars. That is what this design forecloses.
- **The organisational thesis is never tested, neither as a mechanism nor as a result, and this is the criticism the whole note turns on.** On the mechanism side the aggregation is a fixed pipeline described in prose, analyst reports to researchers, debate outcome to trader, proposal to risk team, discussion to manager, with no weight anywhere, no confidence, no way for an uninformative channel to be discounted, and a facilitator that picks the prevailing perspective under no stated criterion. On the evidence side nothing is ablated, seven roles plus a debate, a facilitator, a three-way risk deliberation, a structured protocol and a two-tier backbone are evaluated as one block, and every baseline is a non-LLM rule, so the comparison cannot separate the firm structure from the debate from the risk team from the plain fact that an LLM is reading news and filings the rules never see. <font color="#ff0000">A single-agent LLM baseline would have cost one more run and would have carried most of the paper's weight</font>, and its absence means the title's claim rests on no evidence in either direction.
- **No execution friction of any kind.** Not a flat fee, not a spread, not slippage, not impact, on a system that takes daily long and short positions and whose transaction-history figures show frequent reversals. Against the rest of my set this is the extreme of the distribution, [[MacroHFT, Memory Augmented Context-aware Reinforcement Learning On High Frequency Trading]] at a flat 0.02 %, Jung and Lee at 0.1 % fee plus 0.1 % slippage, [[FinHEAR, Human Expertise and Adaptive Risk-Aware Temporal Reasoning for Financial Decision-Making]] at a single 5 bps check, and all three of those are already generous for anything thinner than US large caps. A 26 % return on a zero-cost book is not a return figure, it is an upper bound.
- **The backbone almost certainly saw the test period, and the look-ahead control does not address that.** The window is January to March 2024, and `gpt-4o` and `o1-preview` were trained on data well past it. The paper's control is a pipeline control, agents are shown only data dated up to the decision day, but nothing stops the model from knowing how AAPL traded in Q1 2024 from pre-training. The AAPL result is the case where this matters most, since it is the paper's headline, it required correctly shorting a falling large-cap stock, and it is the kind of well-documented price move a general model is most likely to have absorbed. I am not claiming contamination occurred, I am noting that the design cannot distinguish it from skill, and that a test period after the backbone's cutoff would.
- **Reporting gaps that make the run hard to reproduce even with the code.** The debate depth $n$ is never given, temperature and decoding settings are not stated, the five-stock universe does not match the three reported results, and insider data is sourced from SEDI, which is the Canadian filing system, for three US-listed tickers. Individually small, collectively they mean the published numbers cannot be reconstructed from the paper.

#### Why this matters for my project

- **It is the origin of my architecture and it is now the clearest statement of what my architecture has to add.** Three of the four analyst roles map onto my set, the technical analyst onto $A_T$, the fundamentals analyst onto $A_F$ once its inputs become CSE annual reports, quarterlies and announcements through structure-aware retrieval, and the sentiment and news analysts onto nothing, since sentiment is out of scope. What survives the mapping is role specialisation by evidence type. What does not survive is the firm metaphor, because the hierarchy is doing the coordination work and a hierarchy cannot condition on market state. The one-line version for the write-up is that <font color="#ffc000">this paper decomposes the decision and does not recompose it</font>, and the recomposition is my contribution.
- **Their risk team is downstream of the decision and my $A_V$ is upstream of it, which is a design choice I should now argue explicitly rather than assume.** Here the aggressive, neutral and conservative agents review a trading plan the trader has already formed, so risk never enters as evidence, only as a revision. In my design $A_V$ produces volatility and persistence evidence that the orchestrator conditions on through $V_t$ and $P_t$ before any decision exists. The indirect support for the upstream placement is that this framework's drawdowns lose to a plain zero-mean-reversion rule on all three stocks despite a dedicated three-agent risk team, which is consistent with risk-as-review being too late to shape position sizing. Indirect is the right word, no ablation isolates the risk team, so this is a hypothesis this paper motivates rather than a result it delivers.
- **The strongest single argument I have for the orchestrator, and it is a different argument from the other three.** [[A Multimodal Foundation Agent for Financial Trading, Tool-Augmented, Diversified, and Generalist]], [[AlphaAgents, Large Language Model Based Multi-Agents for Equity Portfolio Constructions]] and Jung and Lee each show a fixed-influence component degrading a system, and in each case the damage was measured. TradingAgents is the case where the damage would be invisible, seven channels feeding a hierarchy with no weights and no ablation, so a channel could be contributing nothing on most days and the framework would return the same headline number. That converts the gap from "existing systems weight agents badly" into the sharper "existing systems have no quantity that could be weighted", which is a cleaner motivation for the three-way comparison of equal, static learned and dynamic state-aware weighting.
- **The structured communication protocol is the part to copy, and it is a precondition for my weighting function rather than a nicety.** $w_{i,t} = f(L_t, V_t, P_t, C_{i,t})$ needs agent outputs in a form that can be weighted, and free prose is not it. The commitment this paper argues for is that <font color="#ffff00">each agent emits a structured record, a directional signal, a confidence and a prose rationale, written into a shared state the orchestrator reads</font>, with natural language confined to the rationale field. That gives the orchestrator something numeric to combine and avoids the context accumulation this paper correctly diagnoses, but it leaves the comparable-confidence problem untouched, since a structured slot does not make four heterogeneous producers' confidences mean the same thing.
- **Sixty days is the mistake I am closest to repeating, and it lands directly on my outstanding data problem.** A three-month backtest produced a Sharpe of 8.21 that the authors themselves flag as an artefact of too few pullbacks, which is what a short window does to any volatility-denominated metric. The only confirmed ready-made CSE set covers February 2025 to February 2026, twelve months, and against this example that is a floor I am close to rather than a comfortable margin. The decision it forces is to pre-commit to a minimum estimation window before seeing results, report the window length beside every risk-adjusted figure, and treat a Sharpe outside the ordinary empirical range as a diagnostic that the sample is too short rather than as a result.
- **It anchors the zero end of my friction tally, which is what makes the friction-aware evaluation layer a contribution rather than housekeeping.** A framework reporting 26 % on a daily long-short strategy with no cost term of any kind is the cleanest citation I have for the claim that these systems are designed and tuned where execution cost is a rounding error. The CSE sits well past the other end, higher explicit charges, a spread that is a real cost, and whale concentration that makes impact a separate term rather than something foldable into a spread assumption.[^1] [[HLOB – Information persistence and structure in limit order books]] and [[A reinforcement learning approach to optimal execution]] carry the mechanism, this one carries the motivation.
- **The appendix transcript is the template for the decision-log half of the explainability work.** My attribution stack after the re-scope is RAG citation tracing, SHAP over risk features, interpretable FIGARCH and GJR-GARCH parameters and the orchestrator's weight vector, all of which are component-level. What this paper adds is that the end-to-end log is itself a deliverable and costs nothing, because the reasoning is produced as a by-product. Writing one full CSE trading day into the evaluation chapter, from each agent's structured record through the weight vector to the final position, is cheap and does more for a reader than any of the component metrics.
- **The inference-cost figure is a planning constraint I should size now rather than discover.** Eleven LLM calls and over twenty tool calls per trading day is what limited this paper to sixty days. Four agents plus an orchestrator over roughly 250 CSE trading days, run repeatedly across three weighting schemes and four state-input ablations, multiplies out fast, and the expensive component is $A_F$'s retrieval over filings. Since CSE annual reports and quarterlies change on a quarterly cadence rather than daily, caching the fundamental agent's retrieval per reporting period instead of per day is the obvious saving, and it should go into the experimental design before the run rather than after the budget runs out.

[^1]: Whales, holders with positions large enough to move price by trading them. The concentration is a standing reason the CSE is not just a smaller version of a deep market, and it is why impact has to be modelled separately rather than folded into a spread assumption. ^b7c914

### What the Student Guide settles

- **The recommended presentation structure is slides 1 to 20**, mapping exactly onto the template, with a rubric link given per slide. That mapping is reproduced below and supersedes my own.
- **Section 22 lists sixteen questions students should be prepared to answer.** Every one of them is already covered by a note in this folder, which is a useful check rather than a coincidence. They belong in note 22.
- **The guide does not name a referencing style.** Its readiness checklist says only that citations and the reference list must be accurate and complete, so the Cite Them Right Harvard default in note 21 stands, still to be confirmed.

> [!WARNING]
> **The two issued documents conflict on duration, and it changes the deck's time budget.**
>

Everything in this folder was planned against the rubric's stricter reading, which is the safe direction to be wrong in, but it is worth one question to the supervisor before rehearsing.

### What the corpus probe did and did not establish

**Established.** Publication dates are present and are announcement dates rather than reporting-period ends, so the as-of cursor filters correctly. The documents carry a text layer, so no OCR stage is required on the sampled range.

**Not established.** Usable coverage on numerically demanding questions. That remains a hypothesis to measure with a probe set, and the benchmark figure from other financial-QA pipelines must not be quoted as a CSE result. The probe is 22 files out of 22,893, chosen across four counters and fifteen years, which supports a scoped claim and not a corpus-wide one.

## One standing warning before lifting text into slides

[[ELI5 - The Whole Project in Plain English]] is the most quotable note in the vault and is **superseded on data**. It still states 374 trading days per counter, 270 companies over ten years, and trading-activity data as missing. All three were corrected between 18 and 21 October: roughly 356 equity securities, about 853,535 traded rows, 3,920 validated trading days from 2010 to 2026-06-30, median per-counter history around 3,400 days, and turnover and trade count held across the full span.

This is not only a number. The forecast-combination objection in that note leans on 374 days to argue that limited data favours equal weights, and [[Research Matrices Status]] already records that the argument "is weaker than it reads" at 3,400 days. If that passage reaches a slide unrevised, it presents a weakened version of my own strongest counter-argument.

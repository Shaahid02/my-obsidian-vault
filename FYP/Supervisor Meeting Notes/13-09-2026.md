# G1 — Execution friction

### Current claim

> “Nobody has put a liquidity-aware execution model underneath an LLM multi-agent trading framework…”

This is **too strong now**.

Recent research explicitly identifies realistic market-impact modelling as an open weakness of agentic trading, but there are also already developments addressing it. A 2026 work introduces nonlinear market-impact environments using Almgren–Chriss and square-root impact models for RL trading.

More importantly, a **peer-reviewed 2026 multi-agent LLM trading paper** evaluates its system with **slippage and transaction costs explicitly included**.

So:

> **“LLM multi-agent systems do not consider trading friction”**

is no longer true.

However, there is still a potentially strong gap:

> **Existing agentic trading frameworks inadequately model state-dependent liquidity and execution friction characteristic of thin frontier equity markets such as the CSE.**

That is much more defensible.

The novelty would have to come from something such as:

$$ExecutionCost_t = f\left( \frac{OrderSize_t}{ADV_t}, Amihud_t, Volatility_t, Turnover_t \right)$$

rather than simply:

$$Cost = 0.002\times TradeValue$$

If the student genuinely develops and validates a **CSE-calibrated liquidity-sensitive execution mechanism**, G1 remains strong.

### Verdict

**Genuine integration/domain gap: YES.  
Globally unique problem: NO.  
Potentially novel CSE mechanism: YES.**

# G2 — Volatility memory as an operational risk guardrail

This one is more interesting.

The student correctly identified that recent CSE research establishes **long-memory volatility using ARMA-FIGARCH**. The study specifically reports persistent long-memory behaviour in the CSE and discusses implications for risk management.

But the statement:

> “volatility long memory is measured but never used”

is too broad globally.

FIGARCH and related long-memory models have already been used for:

- volatility forecasting,
- VaR,
- portfolio risk,
- hedging,
- asset allocation.

This is established research, not a new methodology.

What may actually be novel is this:

> **Using CSE-estimated volatility persistence to dynamically modify risk exposure or agent influence inside an LLM/multi-agent trading architecture.**

That is an **integration gap**, not a FIGARCH research gap.

There is another conceptual problem in the sheet.

The fractional parameter dd itself is not automatically a rapidly changing “live risk signal.” It measures persistence. If the student wants dynamic orchestration, they need to consider:

$$d_t$$

estimated through rolling/regime-sensitive modelling, or combine it with forecast conditional volatility:

$$\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{t+1}$$

Then the orchestration might become:

$$RiskBudget_t = g(d_t,\hat{\sigma}_{t+1},Liquidity_t)$$

That is substantially stronger research.

### Verdict

**Genuine integration gap: YES.  
Unique FIGARCH application: NO.  
Potentially novel agentic risk-control mechanism: YES.**

I would keep this.

# G3 — “There is no Sinhala financial-sentiment resource”

This statement is **factually too strong**.

There is already 2025 work specifically on **Sinhala, English and code-mixed sentiment in a financial/banking context**, using XLM-RoBERTa, lexicon integration, SHAP and LIME.

There are also existing Sinhala-English code-mixed sentiment datasets, and in July 2026 **SalAngaBhava**, a Sinhala aspect-based sentiment dataset, was published.

So:

> ❌ “No Sinhala financial sentiment resource exists.”

should be removed.

But this narrower statement may still be defensible:

> **There appears to be no established benchmark dataset specifically for Sinhala/Singlish/code-mixed equity-market sentiment associated with CSE companies and investment discussions.**

That is much stronger.

And it matters because **banking customer satisfaction sentiment ≠ stock-market sentiment**.

For example:

> “Commercial Bank service is terrible.”

Negative consumer sentiment.

But:

> “COMB profits will beat estimates after rate cuts.”

Potentially positive equity-market sentiment.

Different task.

### Verdict

This is primarily a:

**dataset gap + domain-adaptation gap.**

It can be a worthwhile contribution, but I would **not make dataset creation one of the three main theoretical contributions** unless the research itself is primarily NLP.

### Revised novelty

**3–4/5**, not 5/5.

# G4 — Structure-aware financial RAG only tested on SEC filings

This is the biggest problem in the sheet.

### The claim is wrong.

There is already **2025 work specifically on CSE annual reports**:

**“Neo4j-powered Graph-RAG system for financial insights on the Colombo Stock Exchange.”**

The researchers transform **CSE annual reports** into a Neo4j financial knowledge graph and combine it with an LLM-driven Graph-RAG pipeline.

That paper is extremely close to the student's proposed CSE fundamental/RAG component.

So the statement:

> “CSE annual reports … nobody has checked whether any of it transfers”

cannot remain.

Additionally, RAG over company annual reports is already an active field beyond the original FinanceBench work. Recent research evaluates RAG directly over company annual reports and develops increasingly structure-aware financial QA systems.

### Therefore G4 is not a core gap.

The student can absolutely use CSE annual-report RAG, but it should be treated as an **existing technological component**.

For example:

> Fundamental Agent  
> → Graph-RAG / multimodal RAG  
> → CSE annual reports

That is fine.

But don't claim RAG itself as novel.

### Potential small gap

The student could investigate:

> **table-aware/multimodal numerical QA reliability on heterogeneous CSE annual reports**

against:

- vanilla RAG,
- Graph-RAG,
- multimodal RAG.

But even this would be a **supporting experiment**, not the central novelty.

### Verdict

**Remove G4 as a major research gap.**

This finding also tells me that the student's literature search is **not yet sufficiently complete**, because a directly relevant **2025 CSE Graph-RAG paper** was missed.

# G5 — Developed-market sentiment relationships do not transfer to CSE

There is a legitimate research question here, but the gap is overstated.

The CSE-specific 2025 liquidity study does indeed find relationships that differ from developed-market expectations. It reports, among other things, a positive relationship between turnover-based sentiment and expected CSE returns.

However, CSE + sentiment is **not an unexplored area**.

There is a 2023 paper:

**Stock Movement Prediction With Sentiment Analysis Based on Grey Exponential Smoothing Method: A Case Study on Colombo Stock Exchange, Sri Lanka**

using social-media sentiment with CSE prediction.

There is also 2024 work integrating sentiment and predictive modelling for CSE sectors.

Therefore:

> “Nobody has put text sentiment next to CSE…”

needs considerable qualification.

### Better research question

Instead of treating this as a separate gap, I would turn it into:

> **Can an agent learn the market-conditional predictive value and direction of local sentiment rather than assuming a fixed mapping between positive sentiment and future returns?**

That is much better.

For example:

$$w_{sentiment,t} = f( Liquidity_t, Volatility_t, Regime_t, SentimentReliability_t )$$

Now sentiment has **dynamic importance**, rather than:

> positive → BUY  
> negative → SELL.

That begins to contribute to the **orchestration problem**, which is where I think this research should go.

### Verdict

Good **research question / subproblem**.

Not strong enough as an independent primary gap.

# G6 — Explainability

The underlying problem is genuine:

> an LLM-generated explanation is not necessarily a faithful explanation of what caused the decision.

That is a valid concern.

But explainable multi-agent trading is no longer unexplored.

A peer-reviewed 2026 paper specifically proposes an **explainable multi-agent LLM trading architecture**, evaluates rationales, and uses G-EVAL for interpretability.

Other 2026 work is also explicitly studying explainability and policy stability in orchestrated trading agents.

Therefore the sheet's:

> “Layered per-component attribution … is new”

cannot be assumed.

Combining:

- SHAP,
- LIME,
- econometric coefficients,
- RAG citations,
- agent transcripts

is useful engineering, but **combining existing XAI tools does not automatically constitute a research gap**.

### Verdict

Treat G6 as:

> **design requirement / supporting contribution**

not a central novelty.

# G7 — Evaluation discipline

The sheet itself already partly recognises the answer:

> “Not novel as a method.”

Correct.

This is **not a research gap that should appear alongside G1–G6 as a proposed contribution**.

It is a **mandatory research methodology requirement**.

Recent 2026 literature is itself criticising LLM trading studies for inconsistent:

- execution assumptions,
- temporal splitting,
- transaction-cost modelling,
- turnover treatment,
- held-out evaluation,
- reproducibility.

So the student absolutely should use:

- walk-forward evaluation,
- transaction costs,
- slippage,
- multiple regimes,
- ablation,
- significance testing,
- buy-and-hold baselines,
- risk-adjusted metrics.

But that is **how the research should be evaluated**, not what the research contribution is.

### Verdict

Move G7 out of the Research Gap table.

Put it under:

> **Evaluation Methodology / Experimental Protocol**

# What I think has happened

The student has taken **limitations found in papers** and treated almost every limitation as a **research gap**.

That is not the same thing.

For example:

> Existing paper does not use SHAP.

does not automatically mean:

> “Lack of SHAP is a research gap.”

Likewise:

> Existing TradingAgents does not use FIGARCH.

does not automatically mean:

> “FIGARCH + TradingAgents is novel.”

Otherwise research becomes:

> A + B + C + D have not previously been combined.

That is **integration**, not necessarily research novelty.

This is especially important under your 90+ criterion.

A 90+ project should not be:

> **TradingAgents + FIGARCH + Sinhala sentiment + Graph-RAG + SHAP.**

That is predominantly **integration of existing work**.

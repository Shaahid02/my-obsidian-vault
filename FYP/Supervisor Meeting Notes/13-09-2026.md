## 3. Detailed Gap Analysis

### 3.1 G1 — Execution Friction

**Current claim (too strong):**

> "Nobody has put a liquidity-aware execution model underneath an LLM multi-agent trading framework…"

Recent research explicitly identifies realistic market-impact modelling as an open weakness of agentic trading, but developments already address it. A 2026 work introduces nonlinear market-impact environments using Almgren–Chriss and square-root impact models for RL trading. More importantly, a **peer-reviewed 2026 multi-agent LLM trading paper** evaluates its system with **slippage and transaction costs explicitly included**.

So *"LLM multi-agent systems do not consider trading friction"* is no longer true.

**Defensible reformulation:**

> Existing agentic trading frameworks inadequately model **state-dependent liquidity and execution friction** characteristic of thin frontier equity markets such as the CSE.

**Where the novelty must come from** — a friction model of the form:

$$ExecutionCost_t = f\left( \frac{OrderSize_t}{ADV_t},\ Amihud_t,\ Volatility_t,\ Turnover_t \right)$$

rather than a flat cost assumption:

$$Cost = 0.002 \times TradeValue$$

If a **CSE-calibrated liquidity-sensitive execution mechanism** is genuinely developed and validated, G1 remains strong.

**Verdict:** Genuine integration/domain gap — YES. Globally unique problem — NO. Potentially novel CSE mechanism — YES.

---

### 3.2 G2 — Volatility Memory as an Operational Risk Guardrail

Recent CSE research correctly establishes **long-memory volatility using ARMA-FIGARCH**, reporting persistent long-memory behaviour in the CSE with implications for risk management.

**Claim that is too broad:**

> "Volatility long memory is measured but never used."

FIGARCH and related long-memory models have already been used for volatility forecasting, VaR, portfolio risk, hedging and asset allocation. This is established research, not a new methodology.

**What may actually be novel:**

> Using CSE-estimated volatility persistence to **dynamically modify risk exposure or agent influence** inside an LLM/multi-agent trading architecture.

That is an **integration gap**, not a FIGARCH research gap.

**Conceptual correction.** The fractional parameter $d$ is not automatically a fast-moving "live risk signal" — it measures persistence. Dynamic orchestration requires either a rolling/regime-sensitive estimate $d_t$, or combination with forecast conditional volatility $\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{t+1}$, giving:

$$RiskBudget_t = g(d_t,\ \hat{\sigma}_{t+1},\ Liquidity_t)$$

**Verdict:** Genuine integration gap — YES. Unique FIGARCH application — NO. Potentially novel agentic risk-control mechanism — YES. **Keep this.**

---

### 3.3 G3 — Sinhala Financial Sentiment

**Claim that is factually too strong:**

> ❌ "No Sinhala financial sentiment resource exists."

Existing resources include:

- 2025 work on **Sinhala, English and code-mixed sentiment in a financial/banking context**, using XLM-RoBERTa, lexicon integration, SHAP and LIME.
- Existing Sinhala-English code-mixed sentiment datasets.
- **SalAngaBhava** (July 2026), a Sinhala aspect-based sentiment dataset.

**Defensible reformulation:**

> There appears to be no established **benchmark dataset specifically for Sinhala/Singlish/code-mixed equity-market sentiment** associated with CSE companies and investment discussions.

This matters because **banking customer-satisfaction sentiment ≠ stock-market sentiment**:

| Text                                                | Consumer sentiment | Equity-market sentiment |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| "Commercial Bank service is terrible."              | Negative           | Not necessarily bearish |
| "COMB profits will beat estimates after rate cuts." | Neutral            | Potentially bullish     |

Different task.

**Verdict:** Primarily a **dataset gap + domain-adaptation gap**. Worthwhile, but should **not** be one of the three main theoretical contributions unless the research is primarily NLP. **Revised novelty: 3–4/5, not 5/5.**

---

### 3.4 G4 — Structure-Aware Financial RAG "Only Tested on SEC Filings"

**This is the biggest problem in the sheet — the claim is wrong.**

2025 work exists specifically on CSE annual reports: **"Neo4j-powered Graph-RAG system for financial insights on the Colombo Stock Exchange"**, which transforms CSE annual reports into a Neo4j financial knowledge graph combined with an LLM-driven Graph-RAG pipeline. That paper is extremely close to the proposed CSE fundamental/RAG component.

The statement *"CSE annual reports … nobody has checked whether any of it transfers"* cannot remain. RAG over company annual reports is also an active field well beyond the original FinanceBench work.

**Consequence.** CSE annual-report RAG may still be used, but as an **existing technological component**:

> Fundamental Agent → Graph-RAG / multimodal RAG → CSE annual reports

Do not claim RAG itself as novel.

**Possible small supporting experiment:** table-aware/multimodal numerical QA reliability on heterogeneous CSE annual reports, benchmarked against vanilla RAG, Graph-RAG and multimodal RAG. Supporting only, not central novelty.

**Verdict:** **Remove G4 as a major research gap.** This finding also indicates the literature search is not yet complete.

### 3.5 G5 — Developed-Market Sentiment Relationships Do Not Transfer to CSE

There is a legitimate research question here, but the gap is overstated. The CSE-specific 2025 liquidity study does find relationships that differ from developed-market expectations — including a positive relationship between turnover-based sentiment and expected CSE returns.

However, CSE + sentiment is **not unexplored**:

- 2023 — *Stock Movement Prediction With Sentiment Analysis Based on Grey Exponential Smoothing Method: A Case Study on Colombo Stock Exchange, Sri Lanka* (social-media sentiment + CSE prediction).
- 2024 — work integrating sentiment and predictive modelling for CSE sectors.

So *"Nobody has put text sentiment next to CSE…"* needs considerable qualification.

**Better research question:**

> Can an agent **learn the market-conditional predictive value and direction** of local sentiment, rather than assuming a fixed mapping between positive sentiment and future returns?

$$w_{sentiment,t} = f( Liquidity_t,\ Volatility_t,\ Regime_t,\ SentimentReliability_t )$$

Sentiment then has **dynamic importance** rather than a fixed `positive → BUY` / `negative → SELL` rule — which feeds directly into the orchestration problem.

**Verdict:** Good research question / subproblem. Not strong enough as an independent primary gap.

---

### 3.6 G6 — Explainability

The underlying problem is genuine: *an LLM-generated explanation is not necessarily a faithful explanation of what caused the decision.*

But explainable multi-agent trading is no longer unexplored. A peer-reviewed 2026 paper proposes an **explainable multi-agent LLM trading architecture**, evaluates rationales and uses G-EVAL for interpretability; other 2026 work studies explainability and policy stability in orchestrated trading agents.

Therefore *"Layered per-component attribution … is new"* cannot be assumed. Combining SHAP, LIME, econometric coefficients, RAG citations and agent transcripts is useful engineering, but **combining existing XAI tools does not automatically constitute a research gap**.

**Verdict:** Treat G6 as a **design requirement / supporting contribution**, not a central novelty.

---

### 3.7 G7 — Evaluation Discipline

The sheet already partly recognises this ("Not novel as a method") — correct. This is a **mandatory research methodology requirement**, not a contribution.

Recent 2026 literature criticises LLM trading studies for inconsistent execution assumptions, temporal splitting, transaction-cost modelling, turnover treatment, held-out evaluation and reproducibility. The project must therefore use walk-forward evaluation, transaction costs, slippage, multiple regimes, ablation, significance testing, buy-and-hold baselines and risk-adjusted metrics.

But that is **how the research is evaluated**, not what it contributes.

**Verdict:** Move G7 out of the Research Gap table into **Evaluation Methodology / Experimental Protocol**.

---

### 3.8 Core Methodological Criticism

Limitations found in papers have been treated as research gaps. These are not equivalent:

| Observed limitation | Invalid inference |
|---|---|
| Existing paper does not use SHAP | "Lack of SHAP is a research gap" |
| TradingAgents does not use FIGARCH | "FIGARCH + TradingAgents is novel" |

Otherwise research reduces to *"A + B + C + D have not previously been combined"* — which is **integration**, not necessarily research novelty.

This matters especially under the 90+ criterion. A 90+ project should not be **TradingAgents + FIGARCH + Sinhala sentiment + Graph-RAG + SHAP**.

## 4. Revised Research Direction

### 4.1 Main Research Gap

> **Existing multi-agent financial decision systems are predominantly developed and evaluated in highly liquid or structurally different markets, while limited research investigates how heterogeneous financial agents should be dynamically coordinated when evidence reliability, execution feasibility and risk change under the liquidity constraints and persistent volatility characteristics of thin frontier equity markets such as the Colombo Stock Exchange.**

Note what this formulation deliberately **does not** claim — each of these would be vulnerable:

- The CSE is irrational.
- Nobody has researched the CSE.
- Nobody has used multi-agent systems.
- Nobody has modelled volatility.
- Nobody has included transaction costs.

### 4.2 Gap Hierarchy

| Level | Gap | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **MAIN GAP** | Lack of state-aware adaptive orchestration for multi-agent financial decision-making under frontier-market liquidity and volatility conditions | Primary technical BoK contribution |
| **Sub-gap 1** | Existing agentic trading environments inadequately represent state-dependent liquidity/execution friction in thin frontier markets | CSE-calibrated friction mechanism |
| **Sub-gap 2** | Persistent/long-memory volatility information is not systematically incorporated into dynamic agent weighting and risk control | Volatility-memory-aware orchestration |

Everything else is repositioned:

| Former gap | New role |
|---|---|
| Sinhala/CSE sentiment | Specialised input agent + possible dataset contribution |
| CSE annual-report RAG | Existing fundamental-analysis component |
| Explainability | Architectural requirement |
| Walk-forward / significance testing | Experimental protocol |
| Sentiment-return relationship | Experiment determining how the sentiment agent should be weighted |

This gives the research **one coherent story** instead of seven loosely connected "gaps".

### 4.3 Formalising the Contribution

Market-state vector driving orchestration:

$$W_t = f( L_t,\ V_t,\ d_t,\ C_t,\ R_t )$$

where:

- $L_t$ = liquidity state
- $V_t$ = forecast volatility
- $d_t$ = volatility persistence
- $C_t$ = agent confidence
- $R_t$ = market regime

Instead of fixed agent influence:

$$Decision = w_T T + w_F F + w_S S + w_R R$$

the weights become dynamic:

$$w_{i,t} = f(L_t,\ V_t,\ d_t,\ C_{i,t},\ Regime_t)$$

$$Decision_t = \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{i,t} A_{i,t} \qquad \text{subject to} \qquad \sum_i w_{i,t} = 1$$

rather than simply letting an LLM manager read five recommendations:

$$Decision_t = LLM(A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4)$$

**Resulting research question:**

> Does adaptive reliability-weighted orchestration outperform static agent fusion under changing frontier-market conditions?

That is a genuine ML/agentic research question and a framework-level contribution.

### 4.4 Sub-Gap 1 — Liquidity-Aware Execution and Decision Feasibility

> Existing multi-agent financial systems inadequately represent state-dependent liquidity and execution constraints characteristic of thin frontier markets, causing strategy evaluation and agent recommendations potentially to assume trades that may be unrealistic under CSE trading conditions.

Proposed CSE-sensitive friction model:

$$F_t = f( RelativeVolume_t,\ Turnover_t,\ Amihud_t,\ OrderSize_t/ADV_t,\ Volatility_t )$$

If proper order-book/bid-ask data is unavailable, this must be explicitly described as a **proxy-based liquidity-friction model**, with the proxies validated.

### 4.5 Sub-Gap 2 — Volatility/Liquidity-Aware Agent Reliability

> Can long-memory/persistence and current volatility state dynamically modify the confidence, weighting, routing or risk budget of heterogeneous agents?

$$w_{i,t} = f( Liquidity_t,\ \hat{\sigma}_{t+1},\ Persistence_t,\ Regime_t,\ Confidence_{i,t} )$$

### 4.6 Sentiment Under the New Framework

The assumption that "the CSE is sentiment-driven" must **not be accepted as fact — it must be tested**.

Hypothesis to investigate:

| Market state | Expected dominant signal |
|---|---|
| High liquidity + low volatility | Technical / fundamental signals dominate |
| Low liquidity + high retail activity | Sentiment becomes disproportionately influential |

The framework could learn $w_{sentiment,t} \uparrow$ under one market condition and $w_{sentiment,t} \downarrow$ under another. If the evidence supports this, the project has **discovered something about the CSE** rather than assuming it beforehand. That distinction is very important.

### 4.7 Justifying "Why the CSE?"

**Not acceptable:**

> "Because the CSE is different and not many people research it."

**Correct framing:**

> The CSE is selected as a frontier-market case because its lower liquidity, heterogeneous trading activity and documented volatility persistence provide a demanding environment in which to test whether financial-agent orchestration methods developed primarily in different market structures remain robust. The research therefore uses the CSE not merely as a new dataset, but as a **stress case** for investigating adaptive multi-agent decision-making under market-state constraints.

| **G2 — Volatility-memory risk guardrail** | Long historical ASPI/S&P SL20 daily prices across normal, COVID and crisis regimes | Relatively obtainable | 🟢 **Most data-feasible core gap** |

| **G4 — CSE financial-report RAG** | CSE annual/quarterly reports + manually constructed QA/evidence benchmark | Reports likely available; benchmark must be created | 🟢/🟠 |

| **G7 — Rigorous evaluation** | Long CSE price/volume history for walk-forward testing across multiple regimes | **Not established** | 🔴 **Critical project gate** |

### 6.2 Required Dataset Stack

| ID | Dataset / Corpus | Role in research | Essential variables | Labels? | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | CSE individual-stock historical market data | Technical signals, backtesting, portfolio decisions | Date, ticker, open, high, low, close, volume, turnover | No | **Critical** |
| **D2** | CSE liquidity / trading-activity data | Liquidity-friction modelling | Volume, turnover, number of trades, market cap; bid/ask/spread/order book if obtainable | No | **Critical** |
| **D3** | ASPI / S&P SL20 historical data | Volatility memory and market-state identification | Index prices, returns, volume if available | No | **Critical** |
| **D4** | CSE annual and quarterly reports | Fundamental / RAG agent | PDFs with financial statements, ratios, commentary, tables | No training labels | High |

### 6.3 D1 — CSE Stock-Market Time Series

Collect **company-level**, not only index-level, data. Minimum structure:

| Date | Ticker | Open | High | Low | Close | Volume | Turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Derivable features: log returns, rolling volatility, momentum, RSI/MACD, moving averages, relative volume, drawdown, abnormal returns, trading intensity.

This is a dataset even though it carries no class labels. **Historical depth is a major requirement — preferably 8–10+ years**, because the work needs multiple market conditions rather than one recent period.

### 6.4 D2 — Liquidity Dataset

**The most important unresolved dataset issue.** Ideally collect:

| Date | Ticker | Volume | Turnover | Number of Trades | Market Cap | Bid | Ask | Spread | Order Depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Historical bid/ask quotes, limit-order-book depth and intraday execution records may be unobtainable. If so, construct **liquidity proxies**:

**Amihud illiquidity**

$$ILLIQ_{i,t} = \frac{|R_{i,t}|}{TradingValue_{i,t}}$$

**Relative volume**

$$RVOL_t = \frac{Volume_t}{\text{Average historical volume}}$$

**Order-size pressure**

$$Pressure_t = \frac{ProposedOrderSize_t}{AverageDailyVolume_t}$$

The research must then describe this as a **proxy-based CSE liquidity/friction model**, not actual order-book execution simulation.

### 6.5 D3 — ASPI and S&P SL20

Required for the **volatility-state / regime component**.

$$r_t = \ln(P_t / P_{t-1})$$

Derived variables: rolling volatility, GARCH conditional volatility, FIGARCH persistence, volatility regimes, drawdown regimes, crisis/normal states.

Supports the research question: *does volatility persistence provide useful information for dynamically modifying agent influence/risk exposure?*

**Note:** using FIGARCH does not by itself constitute developing a new volatility algorithm.

### 6.6 D4 — CSE Annual/Quarterly Reports

These PDFs are also a dataset. Corpus structure:

| Company | Ticker | Year | Report Type | PDF | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

At ~20 companies × 8 years of annual + quarterly reports, this already yields hundreds of documents feeding the fundamental/RAG agent.

**But possessing PDFs does not evaluate RAG.** A separate benchmark is required:

| Question | Company | Year | Gold Answer | Evidence Page | Evidence Text/Table |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

e.g. *"What was Company X's revenue for FY2025?"* — allowing objective comparison of retrieval/QA methods.

### 6.7 D5 — Local CSE Sentiment Corpus

Potential sources: CSE-related public discussion forums, financial news, company-related public discussions, Reddit (if adequate Sri Lankan CSE content exists), other appropriately accessible public social-media sources.

| Date/Time | Text | Source | Language | Ticker | Company |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Timestamp and ticker mapping are extremely important.** A post such as *"I think JKH is going to move strongly tomorrow"* is useful only if when it was posted, which company it concerns, and what subsequent market movement occurred are all known.

### 6.8 D6 — Labelled CSE Financial Sentiment Dataset

Required only if a supervised sentiment classifier is trained.

| ID | Text | Language | Ticker | Date | Annotator 1 | Annotator 2 | Final Sentiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Preferred classes:

$$\{\text{Bullish},\ \text{Neutral},\ \text{Bearish}\}$$

**Bullish/Neutral/Bearish is preferred over generic Positive/Neutral/Negative** because this is financial-market sentiment. Example: *"The company is cutting expenditure drastically"* reads as negative in generic sentiment, but may be bullish for investors if cost reductions improve profitability. This is precisely why generic Sinhala sentiment datasets may be insufficient.

### 6.9 D7 — The Integrated Research Dataset

The real research will operate on a **temporally aligned multimodal dataset** derived from D1–D6:

| Date | Ticker | Return | Volume | Turnover | Liquidity | Volatility | Volatility Persistence | Technical Signal | Fundamental Signal | Sentiment | Sentiment Confidence | Regime | Subsequent Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

This shifts the question from *"Is sentiment useful?"* to:

$$Impact_{sentiment} = f( Liquidity,\ Volatility,\ MarketRegime )$$

Sentiment may work strongly when liquidity is low, participation is concentrated and volatility is elevated, and less well under other states — **that should be discovered from the data, not assumed.**

This integrated dataset is what enables the real research question:

> **Does the importance/reliability of different financial agents change according to CSE liquidity and volatility state?**

### 6.10 Dataset-to-Research-Element Mapping

| Proposed research element | Required datasets |
|---|---|
| Liquidity-aware friction | D1 + D2 |
| Volatility-memory mechanism | D1 + D3 |
| Local sentiment agent | D5 + D6 |
| Fundamental RAG agent | D4 |
| Sentiment × liquidity × return investigation | D1 + D2 + D5/D6 |
| **Adaptive agent orchestration** | **D7** — created by integrating the others |
| Portfolio/backtesting evaluation | D1 + D2 + D3 + D7 |

### 6.11 Build vs. Obtain

**Do not create everything from scratch.**

| Obtain from existing/official sources | Create yourself |
|---|---|
| CSE historical prices; ASPI; S&P SL20; volume; turnover; trade count; market capitalisation; annual reports; quarterly reports | CSE-specific labelled sentiment dataset (see §7 caveat); RAG evaluation QA set; **integrated ticker-date research dataset**; derived liquidity/volatility/regime variables |

**Expected deliverable shape:**

| Dataset | Content |
|---|---|
| **A — CSE Market Dataset** | 20–50 companies × 8–15 years of daily market data |
| **B — CSE Index/Market-State Dataset** | ASPI + S&P SL20 + market statistics over the same period |
| **C — CSE Financial Report Corpus** | Annual/quarterly reports for the selected companies |
| **D — CSE Local Financial Sentiment Dataset** | Collected and manually labelled (scope to be minimised — see §7) |
| **E — Integrated Research Dataset** | A + B + C-derived signals + D, aligned by **ticker + date** |

The **only major datasets genuinely needing creation from scratch are the local CSE sentiment corpus and the final integrated research dataset**; most raw financial data should come from existing authoritative sources.

## 7. Sentiment Dataset — Revised, More Cautious Position

The standing recommendation to **avoid primary dataset collection** applies here, and the earlier position is revised. D5 and D6 should not be treated the same way as D7.

| Dataset | Practical? | Primary collection/annotation risk | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D5/D6 — CSE Sinhala/Singlish/English sentiment data** | Possible, but risky and time-consuming | **High** | **Do not make mandatory** unless no acceptable existing/adaptable dataset exists |
| **D7 — Final integrated ticker-date dataset** | Yes, very practical | Low | **Construct this** |

### 7.1 Why D5/D6 Is Risky

Creating a proper CSE-specific financial sentiment dataset requires far more than scraping posts:

1. Collect suitable CSE-related text
2. Remove spam/duplicates
3. Map posts to companies/tickers
4. Define the annotation scheme
5. Decide labels (bullish / neutral / bearish)
6. Prepare annotation guidelines
7. Use multiple independent annotators
8. Resolve disagreements
9. Measure inter-annotator agreement
10. Check class imbalance
11. Validate the labels
12. Handle Sinhala/Singlish/code-mixed ambiguity
13. Address ethics, privacy, platform terms and redistribution rights

That can easily become a research project in itself. Annotation is non-trivial — *"JKH is dropping. Good time to buy."* is simultaneously negative on price, bullish in intent, and mixed in framing.

**Therefore: "go and create a new Sinhala/Singlish CSE sentiment dataset" is not the default approach.**

### 7.2 Preferred Order of Approaches

**Option A (best) — Transfer from existing resources.** Use an existing financial-market sentiment dataset to establish the finance-specific classifier, and separately use an existing Sinhala/Singlish sentiment dataset for language adaptation:

$$FinancialSentimentKnowledge + LocalLanguageKnowledge \rightarrow CSE\ Sentiment\ Model$$

Much cleaner than manually annotating thousands of CSE posts.

**Option B — Existing model + zero/few-shot classification.** Since the project is fundamentally about **multi-agent orchestration**, sentiment is only one supporting component. Use a strong existing financial sentiment model, multilingual transformer or LLM-based classifier, and evaluate it on a small manually verified sample:

- Collect 300–500 CSE-related posts
- Manually verify a smaller gold-standard subset
- Evaluate the existing sentiment model
- Perform lightweight fine-tuning only if necessary

### 7.3 A Small Gold-Standard Set Is Acceptable

A **small evaluation dataset** (300–500 CSE posts, independently labelled by two or three annotators) is very different from a 10,000-record training dataset. Report agreement with Cohen's $\kappa$, Fleiss' $\kappa$ or Krippendorff's $\alpha$ as appropriate to the annotation setup.

Purpose: **validation of an existing sentiment model on the CSE domain**, not building a sentiment dataset and model from scratch.

### 7.4 Sentiment May Not Need to Be Sinhala/Singlish at All

The assumption *"CSE sentiment agent → must build Sinhala/Singlish sentiment"* must be challenged. The language distribution of usable CSE discussion should be **measured first**. Illustrative:

| Language | Usable CSE posts | % |
|---|--:|--:|
| English | 8,400 | 76% |
| Singlish | 1,700 | 15% |
| Sinhala script | 700 | 6% |
| Tamil/mixed | 300 | 3% |

If the distribution looks like this, a Sinhala-specific model is difficult to justify and would add unnecessary scope. **The research should follow the evidence, not the planned technology list.**

### 7.5 Position to Record

> **Prefer an existing financial/multilingual sentiment dataset or pretrained model. Construct only a small, independently validated CSE-specific evaluation set if necessary to test domain transfer. A new large manually labelled dataset should be created only if the literature and preliminary experiments demonstrate that existing resources are inadequate.**

The biggest improvement is the move away from:

to the sharper claim that existing systems frequently have **no explicit weighting quantity at all**.

That is the best formulation of the contribution so far. What makes it strong is that it identifies an actual mechanism:

rather than "I will build a multi-agent trading system." The Conditioning Set sheet then independently defines $L_t$, $V_t$, $P_t$, $C_{i,t}$ and an abstention mechanism. **That is the point where this moved from an application architecture into a testable technical research contribution.**

| | |
|---|---|

## 7. Strengthening Sub-gap 1

The way to improve Sub-gap 1 is **not to make a bigger claim**. It should become **narrower, more technical, and experimentally attributable**.

| Current weakness | Improvement | Why it strengthens the research |
|---|---|---|
| "Liquidity modelling" is too broad | Make it specifically **state-dependent execution-cost-aware orchestration** | Gives a precise technical mechanism |
| Transaction costs already exist in literature | Distinguish **ex-post cost deduction** from **cost inside the objective** | Changes the decision process itself |
| Thin-market calibration alone risks being merely domain-specific | Make the framework **market-agnostic**, CSE as stress-test | Restores CS/SE contribution |
| Liquidity looks like a separate financial feature | Use liquidity **twice**: execution cost + reliability of price-derived agents | Integrates it with the main gap |
| Novelty hard to attribute | Add baseline, participation-rate sweep, ablations | Shows exactly what creates any improvement |
| Historical bid/ask/depth may be unavailable | Define a **proxy route** and a **microstructure route** in advance | Feasible even without ideal data |
| Claim still contains absolutes | Bound every novelty claim to the reviewed literature | Much safer examiner defence |

The strongest insight already in the file: **liquidity affects both what a trade costs and how much the system should trust price-derived signals.** Build the sub-gap there.

### 7.1 Change the research question

Not:

> ❌ "How can transaction costs be modelled better?" — that becomes finance/microstructure research.

Instead:

> ✅ **How should a multi-agent decision system alter both execution behaviour and the influence of price-derived agents when market liquidity deteriorates?**

That is directly about software/algorithmic orchestration. Liquidity becomes a **control signal**, not an extra feature:

$$L_t \rightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Execution Cost}_t \\ \text{Reliability of Price-derived Agent}_t \end{cases}$$

### 7.2 Put execution cost inside the decision objective

Probably the strongest single improvement. Instead of choosing a portfolio and then computing

$$\text{Net Return} = \text{Return} - \text{Transaction Cost}$$

the method should be closer to:

$$\max_{a_t} \left[ U(a_t) - \lambda\, C(a_t, L_t) \right]$$

where $a_t$ is the proposed allocation/trade, $U(a_t)$ the expected utility/reward, $L_t$ the measured liquidity state, $C(a_t, L_t)$ the expected execution cost, and $\lambda$ the cost sensitivity.

The system then knows **before deciding**:

> "This rebalance may theoretically improve the portfolio, but current liquidity makes the execution too expensive."

### 7.3 Model participation rate explicitly

$$\rho_t = \frac{Q_t}{ADV_t}$$

where $Q_t$ is intended order quantity and $ADV_t$ average daily volume. Cost then becomes

$$C_t = f(\rho_t,\ L_t) \qquad \text{rather than} \qquad C_t = \text{fixed } 0.1\%$$

and can be swept systematically:

$$\rho \in \{0.01,\ 0.025,\ 0.05,\ 0.10,\ \ldots\}$$

The contribution is not "I invented participation rate" — obviously not. It is:

> **systematically testing whether adaptive orchestration decisions remain beneficial when intended order size becomes materially large relative to available liquidity.**

### 7.4 Couple liquidity to agent reliability

This is where the sub-gap integrates with the main contribution. The Technical Agent generates $A_{T,t}$ with confidence $C_{T,t}$. When liquidity is poor, price information is less reliable — infrequent trading, stale prices, zero-return periods, low volume, large price moves from small trades. So define:

$$C'_{T,t} = C_{T,t} \cdot g(L_t)$$

where $g(L_t)$ decreases as illiquidity increases. Liquidity then affects both:

$$\text{Execution: } C_{\text{execution},t} = f(Q_t, L_t) \qquad\qquad \text{Influence: } w_{T,t} = f(C'_{T,t},\ L_t,\ \ldots)$$

Meaning:

> **Poor liquidity not only makes a trade expensive; it may also make the price-derived evidence supporting that trade less trustworthy.**

### 7.5 Rewritten Sub-gap 1

> **Among the reviewed multi-agent financial decision systems, execution costs are commonly represented as fixed or exogenous penalties, while the reviewed execution-specific literature models market impact separately from heterogeneous-agent orchestration. This leaves an unresolved question of how measured liquidity state should jointly affect expected execution cost and the reliability of price-derived agent signals within a shared decision mechanism. The proposed research therefore investigates a liquidity-aware orchestration layer in which expected transaction cost is incorporated into the decision objective and liquidity state simultaneously conditions the influence assigned to relevant agents.**

Considerably stronger than *"CSE is illiquid and transaction costs are not modelled."*

### 7.6 Claim the joint mechanism, not the parts

Do **not** claim individually as new: Amihud, ADV, participation rate, transaction cost, market impact, liquidity states. Claim the **combination**:

$$L_t \rightarrow \begin{cases} \text{Agent Reliability} \\ \text{Execution Cost} \end{cases} \rightarrow \text{Adaptive Orchestration}$$

### 7.7 Four-way baseline design

| | Configuration |
|---|---|
| $B_1$ | Equal weight + fixed cost |
| $B_2$ | Static learned weight + fixed cost |
| $B_3$ | Dynamic agent weight + ex-post cost |
| $P$ | Dynamic agent weight + liquidity-aware cost **inside** objective |

Then ablate $P - \text{LiquidityReliability}$ and $P - \text{CostObjective}$, to show whether improvement comes from changing agent trust, changing execution decisions, or both.

### 7.8 Bound the data problem in advance

Define two levels so the sub-gap does not collapse if order-book data cannot be obtained:

| Level | Inputs | Name |

| **A — guaranteed feasible** | Volume, ADV, zero-volume frequency, zero-return frequency, turnover where available, Amihud where available | **proxy-based liquidity-aware execution model** |

Because this is CS/SE, showing the mechanism is **not dependent on the CSE** would strengthen it considerably: one liquid-market dataset and one thin/frontier dataset, or a normal-liquidity vs artificially stressed scenario, then test whether $\Delta\text{Performance}_{\text{liquid}}$ differs from $\Delta\text{Performance}_{\text{thin}}$. ## 8. Does a 3.7/5 Sub-gap Prevent a 90+ Proposal?

**No.** A 3.7/5 supporting sub-gap does not prevent a 90+ Project Proposal. What matters is **what role that gap plays in the overall research argument**.

The rubric does not require every gap to be 4.5/5. It assesses problem/goal, background evidence, requirements and indicative solution, methodology, feasibility/risk, responsible practice, scope, academic practice and communication. The largest criterion is **Initial Requirements and Indicative Solution at 20%**, then **Methodology and Development Approach at 15%**.

**Structure the thesis as:**

| Role | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Primary contribution** | Adaptive state-aware orchestration of heterogeneous agents |
| **Supporting mechanism A** | Liquidity-aware reliability and execution-cost representation |
| **Supporting mechanism B** | Persistence-aware exposure/risk control |

This is already the direction of the Research Gap document.

### 8.1 What a 90+ proposal needs

| Area | Expectation |
|---|---|
| **Main research gap** | Very clearly evidenced and technically precise |
| **Novelty** | Explicitly differentiated from closest prior work |
| **Literature** | Critical synthesis, not descriptive summaries |
| **Requirements** | Fully traceable from evidence/gap to acceptance criteria |
| **Architecture** | Every major design decision justified by RQs/requirements |
| **Methodology** | Research methodology separated from development methodology |
| **Evaluation** | Strong baselines, ablations, metrics, validity controls |
| **Dataset strategy** | Provenance, quality, limitations and legality clearly defended |
| **PoC** | Demonstrates the actual proposed mechanism is feasible |
| **Scope** | Ambitious but controlled; no feature stuffing |
| **Risk/ethics** | Project-specific, not generic |
| **Presentation** | Extremely clear defence of problem → gap → contribution → method → evidence |

The Student Guide explicitly says strong scope comes from meaningful SE and evaluation rather than feature accumulation.

### 8.2 What would actually hurt

The danger is **not** that Sub-gap 1 is 3.7. The danger is presenting it as *"a major original contribution"* when the literature supports only moderate novelty. That weakens Background and Evidence, Appropriateness of Scope, Academic Practice, Communication, and examiner confidence in the student's judgement.

Saying *"Sub-gap 1 is supportive, previous work already solves part of this, and my contribution is specifically how liquidity is operationalised inside the adaptive orchestration mechanism"* shows **better research judgement** than inflating the novelty.

### 8.3 A modest or negative result is acceptable

If the final experiments show liquidity-aware execution adds only a small benefit, that does not destroy the project. With a justified hypothesis, proper baselines, correct experimentation, ablation, statistical analysis and honest interpretation, the research is still strong.

> Research does not require every proposed enhancement to win. The examiner should see **what was hypothesised, why it was reasonable, how it was tested, what happened, and what was learned.**

### 8.4 Where the 90+ potential actually lies

In the **integration** of the main mechanism —

$$w_{i,t} = f(L_t,\ V_t,\ P_t,\ C_{i,t})$$

— combined with heterogeneous agents, structural confidence, abstention, liquidity-conditioned reliability, volatility persistence, explicit baselines and ablation of each state variable.

Demonstrating **Equal Weight** vs **Static Learned Weight** vs **Dynamic State-Aware Weighting**, then ablating $-L_t$, $-V_t$, $-P_t$, $-C_{i,t}$, makes the contribution **attributable** — which is what makes it defensible.

**Component view:**

| Component | Rating |
|---|--:|
| Main gap | **4.6/5** |
| Persistence sub-gap | **4.5/5** |
| Liquidity sub-gap | **3.7/5** |
| **Overall integration** | **4.8/5** |

That combination is **fully compatible with a very high distinction-level proposal**. But the gap alone only gets the project into the right territory — the difference between 80-level and 90+ will come from **rigour of requirements, methodology, architecture, experimental design, PoC evidence, critical judgement and live defence**.

> **Stop trying to make every component novel.** Make the main orchestration contribution exceptionally well evidenced, implemented and evaluated.
